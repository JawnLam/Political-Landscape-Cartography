# 0651 — THE 'STRATEGIC INCOHERENCE'

> Issuing deliberately vague or contradictory strategic goals, allowing leadership the flexibility to retroactively support successful outcomes and disavow failures.

**Type:** `hybrid` · **Strategy:** **#12 Cost-Focused Triple** · `B(X)↑ + C(X)↓ + C(Y)↑` · **Cluster:** C83 — Strategic Ambiguity, Acting Roles & Ambiguous Authority

**Cited principles:** Baron & Hershey, Baron, Hershey

---

## Classification Rationale

**Ambiguity Source.** The title and description anchor in a specific leadership move — issuing deliberately fuzzy or contradictory strategic direction inside an organization — but the underlying mechanism (preserve interpretive optionality so the principal can post-hoc claim alignment with whichever outcome materialized) is the textbook structure of plausible deniability and operates wherever a principal must commit publicly under uncertainty.

**Specific Interpretation.** Senior leadership crafts the strategic plan, OKR cascade, or transformation charter with intentional vagueness and internal contradictions so that, regardless of which initiatives succeed or fail twelve months later, leadership can map their stated intent onto the winners and disown the losers.

**Generic Interpretation.** A principal facing accountability for an uncertain outcome under-specifies the commitment ex ante, preserving a menu of after-the-fact interpretations. Whatever reality delivers, the principal selects the interpretation that matches it and presents the match as foresight.

**Primary Classification.** generic

**Reasoning.** The corporate-strategy framing is one instance of a much older mechanism — the Delphic oracle's syntactic ambiguity, the central banker's 'constructive ambiguity,' the politician's open-textured campaign promise. What identifies the tactic is the asymmetric option value of vagueness when outcomes are unknown, not the strategy-document setting in which it most often appears in modern firms.

## Specific Analysis

### Choice Architecture

- **Option X:** Subordinates accept the vague/contradictory strategic direction, proceed on their own interpretations, and absorb accountability for outcomes
- **Option Y:** Subordinates push leadership to specify the strategy concretely, name trade-offs, and commit to falsifiable success criteria before execution begins
- **Context:** A senior leadership team issuing a multi-year strategic plan, transformation roadmap, or annual priorities cascade in an environment where market conditions, competitive moves, and execution risk are all uncertain — and where leadership reputation will later be judged against the results

### Active Levers

#### B(X)↑ — Amplify benefit of X — **Active** · intensity 2 (Moderate)

Vague direction gives operators creative latitude — initiative leaders can frame their pet projects as 'in line with the strategy' and claim leadership endorsement, which is a real (if double-edged) benefit of accepting incoherent direction

#### C(X)↓ — Reduce cost of X — **Active** · intensity 3 (Intense)

This is the operating core. Leadership's cost of issuing direction collapses to near zero: no need to make hard trade-offs, no need to defend a specific theory of the business, no exposure to falsification. Accepting the vague direction lets the leadership avoid the political costs of clarity (offending stakeholders whose initiatives don't make the cut)

#### B(Y)↓ — Diminish benefit of Y — Not active

The tactic does not actually argue that clear strategy is unbeneficial — clarity is still socially recognized as virtuous. Leadership operating this tactic typically pays public lip service to clarity while delivering the opposite, rather than attacking the value of clarity directly

#### C(Y)↑ — Amplify cost of Y — **Active** · intensity 3 (Intense)

Pressing leadership for specificity becomes politically costly for subordinates: it reads as questioning leadership competence, exposes the asker to being blamed when the now-specified strategy fails, and breaks the comfortable consensus everyone else is operating under. The cost of demanding clarity is borne by whoever demands it

### Strategy

**#12 Cost-Focused Triple** · `B(X)↑ + C(X)↓ + C(Y)↑`

**Rationale.** The configuration produces the intended shift not by attacking the legitimacy of clear strategy (which would be self-defeating since leadership claims to be providing strategy) but by making vagueness almost free for leadership while loading the cost of demanding clarity onto whoever raises the question. The leader keeps the benefit of looking like a strategist while subordinates absorb the execution risk that specificity would have surfaced.

**Effectiveness factors.** Works when (a) outcomes will take long enough to materialize that the strategy document fades from active memory before judgment, (b) the organization lacks a robust ex-ante commitment-tracking mechanism (red-team review, scenario pre-mortems, signed forecasts), (c) the leader has enough rhetorical skill to make retroactive narrative-fitting sound visionary rather than opportunistic, and (d) no rival is incentivized to maintain a contemporaneous record of what was actually promised. Fails fast under activist-board governance, regulatory disclosure regimes, or in cultures with strong norms of written falsifiable commitment.

## Generic Analysis

### Influence Mechanism

**Psychological Principle.** Eisenberg's Strategic Ambiguity (1984), reinforced by Hindsight Bias (Fischhoff) and Outcome Bias (Baron & Hershey). Eisenberg established that organizational communicators deliberately use ambiguity to preserve interpretive flexibility, maintain plausible deniability, and allow unified-meaning attribution after the fact. Hindsight and outcome bias do the rest: observers reverse-engineer leader intent from whichever outcome appeared, granting credit for foresight that was never actually committed to in advance.

**Decision Alteration.** The mechanism alters the principal-agent accountability calculus by separating commitment from outcome. Under specific commitment, leader reputation is bounded — they get credit only for what they specifically called and lose credit for what they specifically missed. Under deliberate ambiguity, leader reputation becomes unbounded on the upside (every success can be claimed) and floored on the downside (every failure can be disowned as 'not what we really meant'). The decision being altered is the audience's attribution of foresight, not their endorsement of any particular action.

**Optimal Conditions.** Thrives where (a) outcomes are genuinely uncertain so multiple interpretations remain live for long enough to be selected ex post, (b) the principal has narrative control over the post-mortem (no independent historian or transcript), and (c) the audience has cognitive or political incentive to grant retrospective coherence — boards that want to believe their CEO is competent, voters who want to believe they backed a visionary, congregations who want to believe the prophecy was real.

### Representative Scenario 1

**Context:** A central bank chair gives forward guidance using deliberately Delphic phrasing ('data-dependent,' 'gradual,' 'patient') before a major rate decision in a period of conflicting inflation and growth signals

**Sample X:** Audience treats the leader's earlier ambiguous statement as having predicted the outcome that actually occurred

**Sample Y:** Audience holds the leader to the specific, falsifiable interpretation a critic flagged at the time the statement was made

**Template levers:**

- B(X)↑ ✓ — Whatever rate path the bank ultimately takes, the chair can quote prior language showing 'we signaled this all along,' converting policy outcomes into evidence of communication competence regardless of which path was actually expected internally

- C(X)↓ ✓ — Ambiguous forward guidance carries near-zero political cost — no faction is publicly disappointed because every faction can read its preferred reading into the language, and the chair pays no credibility cost for being 'wrong' since they never specified

- B(Y)↓ ✗ — not active in this configuration — the public good of clear central-bank communication is not directly attacked, just structurally avoided

- C(Y)↑ ✓ — A market commentator who insists the chair really meant one specific path is exposed if reality moves the other way, while pundits who hedge alongside the chair face no risk. Demanding specificity becomes professionally hazardous

### Representative Scenario 2

**Context:** A presidential candidate runs on the slogan 'I'll fight for working families' across a year of campaign appearances, declining in every interview to specify which sectors, which trade postures, which tax brackets — and then governs by choosing whichever interpretation matches the bills that pass

**Sample X:** Voters and donors retroactively interpret a candidate's prior campaign statements as having committed to whichever policy positions the elected official now finds politically convenient

**Sample Y:** Voters and donors enforce ex ante the specific reading they relied on at the time of contribution or endorsement

**Template levers:**

- B(X)↑ ✓ — Each successful piece of legislation gets retrofitted as the fulfillment of 'what I always meant by fighting for working families,' compounding the candidate's reputation for delivering on promises despite never having made the specific promise being claimed as kept

- C(X)↓ ✓ — During the campaign, the slogan offended no specific donor coalition; during governance, no specific donor coalition can claim betrayal because no specific commitment was made to them

- B(Y)↓ ✗ — not active in this configuration — the candidate doesn't argue against specificity as such, just declines to provide it while praising 'principles over policy specifics'

- C(Y)↑ ✓ — A journalist who pins the candidate to a specific reading is framed as 'gotcha journalism,' and an activist who holds the candidate to one specific interpretation is told they 'didn't understand the broader vision' — both pay reputational cost for demanding clarity

### Versatility

**Level:** high

**Explanation.** Operates in any context where a principal must make public commitments under uncertainty and where outcomes will be judged after the fact by an audience with imperfect memory or incentive to grant coherence. Documented across corporate strategic planning, central-bank communication, political campaigning, military command intent ('mission-type orders' in their pathological form), religious prophecy, venture-capital thesis statements, and diplomatic communiqués. Less effective in narrow, repeated-game contexts with high-frequency outcome data (e.g., a sales-comp plan judged quarterly), where the gap between commitment and outcome is too small for ambiguity to operate.

### Application Examples

- Corporate: A Fortune 500 CEO issues an annual strategy memo built around 'customer obsession,' 'operational excellence,' and 'innovation' without specifying which trade-offs win when they conflict — then claims credit for whichever P&L line moves favorably
- Government: An administration's national-security strategy document lists every region as 'priority' and every threat as 'pacing,' allowing post-hoc claims that any successful operation was anticipated and any failure was outside the strategy's core focus
- Central banking: A Federal Reserve chair uses 'data-dependent' and 'gradual' as load-bearing phrases in FOMC statements, preserving the option to claim consistency with whatever path emerges from subsequent meetings
- Religious leadership: A televangelist issues prophecy in terms generic enough ('a time of trial is coming,' 'restoration will follow hardship') that any subsequent event can be presented to the congregation as the foretold event
- Diplomacy: A state department issues a joint communiqué with strategically ambiguous language on a contested issue (Taiwan, Kashmir, the Western Sahara) so that domestic audiences in both signatory countries can read it as supporting their preferred interpretation
- Venture capital: A general partner publishes a thesis broad enough ('we back exceptional founders building category-defining companies') that every portfolio win confirms the thesis and every loss is 'an outlier the thesis didn't cover'
- Activism / NGO: A coalition's mission statement is left deliberately broad so that whichever subset of campaigns gains traction can be framed as the movement's flagship work

## Integrated Insights

### Pattern Recognition

Within cluster C83 (Strategic Ambiguity, Acting Roles & Ambiguous Authority), this tactic occupies a distinct slot from its siblings. THE INVISIBLE COMMITTEE diffuses attribution for decisions across an unidentifiable group; THE PERMANENT INTERIM and THE 'ACTING' ROLE TRAP create ambiguity about a person's authority; THE 'AMBIGUOUS AUTHORITY' GRANT creates ambiguity about scope. THE 'STRATEGIC INCOHERENCE' is the only sibling that places the ambiguity in the *content of the direction itself* rather than in the *identity or authority of the director.* The leader is fully present and clearly in charge — what is unstable is the goal-content against which they will later be judged. This makes it harder to counter than the others: you cannot demand to know 'who really decided' because the answer is unambiguously the leader; you can only demand to know 'what they actually meant,' and that question reads as insubordination.

### Strategic Implications

The tactic is asymmetric in time. At the moment of issuance, it appears costless and even sophisticated ('strategic flexibility'). The costs accrue silently: execution layers diverge because they each interpret the direction differently; resource allocation becomes a function of political negotiation rather than strategy; the organization loses the capacity for falsifiable learning because there was never a falsifiable claim to update against. By the time the costs are legible — three to five years downstream, when the firm realizes it has executed in every direction at once and led in none — the leader who deployed the tactic has typically already harvested the reputational benefit and moved on. The countermeasure that actually bites is contemporaneous written commitment with named tradeoffs and pre-registered failure conditions — the strategy-document equivalent of pre-registration in clinical trials. Boards that want clear strategy must demand the artifact, not the rhetoric.

### Resolution Approach

Use the specific lens when diagnosing a particular leadership team's strategy document, OKR cascade, or transformation charter — the question is whether to push back, how, and at what political cost. Use the generic lens when teaching the pattern, designing governance structures resistant to it (pre-mortems, written forecasts, falsifiable commitments), or recognizing the same move in adjacent domains (central banking, electoral politics, prophetic religion, diplomatic communiqué). The generic lens also clarifies why this tactic has a long historical pedigree — the Delphic oracle to the modern CEO are operating the same mechanism — and is therefore unlikely to disappear from any institution that combines public commitment with uncertain outcomes.

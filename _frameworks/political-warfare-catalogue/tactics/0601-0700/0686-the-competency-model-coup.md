# 0686 — THE COMPETENCY MODEL COUP

> Redefining the organization's core competency frameworks or promotion criteria to prioritize skills aligned with the new agenda, instantly rendering the expertise of legacy leaders obsolete.

**Type:** `hybrid` · **Strategy:** **#13 Y-Focused Triple** · `B(X)↑ + B(Y)↓ + C(Y)↑` · **Cluster:** C87 — Skill Obsolescence & Competency Coup

**Cited principles:** Akerlof, Bourdieu, Pfeffer

---

## Classification Rationale

**Ambiguity Source.** The description names a specific organizational artifact (competency frameworks, promotion criteria) and a specific political move (rewriting them to favor the new agenda), but the underlying mechanism — quietly changing the rules of evaluation to invalidate incumbent expertise — is a fundamental influence pattern that recurs anywhere assessment criteria are codified.

**Specific Interpretation.** A regime-change or new-direction leader rewrites the HR competency model (or its equivalent — leadership principles, promotion rubrics, performance review categories) so that the skills most heavily weighted are those the new agenda needs, structurally devaluing the legacy leaders whose careers were built on the prior model.

**Generic Interpretation.** Any influence move that shifts the criteria by which value is judged, so that capabilities previously regarded as expertise are reclassified as 'limitations,' and the would-be challenger's profile becomes the new template for legitimacy.

**Primary Classification.** generic

**Reasoning.** The HR competency-model instantiation is one important venue, but the deeper move — controlling the definition of merit itself — operates identically in academic disciplines redefining what counts as rigor, regulators rewriting compliance standards, and editorial boards revising house style. The mechanism is reusable wherever the rules of evaluation can be rewritten, making the generic reading more fundamental.

## Specific Analysis

### Choice Architecture

- **Option X:** Align with and rapidly demonstrate fluency in the newly promulgated competencies (data literacy, digital fluency, customer-centricity, whatever the new model elevates) — accept that legacy expertise is now a partial credential at best
- **Option Y:** Continue investing in and defending the legacy expertise that was previously rewarded — operational depth, functional specialism, tenure-based judgment — and contest the new model as a fad
- **Context:** A new CEO or transformation leader rolls out a revised competency framework or promotion rubric within the first 12-18 months of arrival. The new model places heavy weight on capabilities the incoming agenda requires (e.g., 'enterprise agility,' 'AI fluency,' 'customer obsession') and quietly drops or downgrades the categories on which the legacy leadership cohort built their advancement

### Active Levers

#### B(X)↑ — Amplify benefit of X — **Active** · intensity 2 (Moderate)

Demonstrating the new competencies becomes the visible path to promotion, plum assignments, and high-potential designation. The benefits are real and structural — the new model encodes them into the HR system — but they accrue mainly on the upside curve, not retroactively, so this lever is clear without being overwhelming

#### C(X)↓ — Reduce cost of X — Not active

Acquiring the new competencies is not made easier — there is no concierge service, no funded retraining, no graceful catch-up program. The tactic deliberately leaves the cost of conversion on the individual, because friction in the conversion path is part of what filters out the legacy cohort. Facilitation is conspicuously absent

#### B(Y)↓ — Diminish benefit of Y — **Active** · intensity 3 (Intense)

This is the operating core. Decades of accumulated expertise — operational knowledge, P&L instinct, deep functional craft — are reclassified, often without explicit demotion, as 'narrow,' 'legacy,' or 'not future-forward.' The same résumé that earned a VP role five years ago now reads as a liability in the new rubric. The tactic does its primary work by destroying the perceived value of the asset legacy leaders have spent their careers accumulating

#### C(Y)↑ — Amplify cost of Y — **Active** · intensity 3 (Intense)

Persisting in the legacy profile now carries hard, visible costs: missed promotions, exclusion from succession slates, lower performance ratings under the new criteria, and the social cost of being publicly marked as someone who 'didn't make the pivot.' The new model translates these costs into HR-system reality — calibration meetings, talent reviews, comp decisions — so the cost of staying with Y compounds at every cycle

### Strategy

**#13 Y-Focused Triple** · `B(X)↑ + B(Y)↓ + C(Y)↑`

**Rationale.** The new agenda is made attractive on the upside (B(X)↑), but the dominant work is done by attacking the alternative on both axes: the legacy expertise is devalued (B(Y)↓ intense) and clinging to it is made career-costly (C(Y)↑ intense). The deliberate absence of facilitation (C(X)↓) is part of the design — the tactic is not trying to retain everyone through the pivot, it is trying to filter and replace, so making the conversion path easy would defeat the underlying purpose. Note the contrast with the 'Velvet Cage' (0032) sibling: that tactic uses Full Spectrum to neutralize a single untouchable; this tactic uses Y-Focused Triple to obsolete an entire cohort by rewriting the rubric they're measured against

**Effectiveness factors.** Success depends on the legitimacy of the rewrite (it must come dressed as 'modernization' or 'future-fitness,' not naked political housecleaning), on capturing HR and the talent-review machinery so the new model has real teeth in calibration and comp, and on the new criteria being defensibly tied to a credible strategic narrative. Common failure modes: the rewrite is too transparently aimed at known individuals (becomes a lawsuit and loses moral authority); the new competencies are so vague they can't be operationalized (legacy leaders just rebrand themselves); or the legacy cohort controls enough board relationships to slow-roll HR adoption until the new CEO loses momentum

## Generic Analysis

### Influence Mechanism

**Psychological Principle.** Bourdieu's theory of cultural capital and field redefinition, combined with Akerlof's information-economics insight on credentialing — when the rules of what counts as legitimate capital change, the holders of the now-deprecated capital experience asset destruction without ever being directly attacked. Pfeffer's analysis of organizational power as the control of definitions reinforces the same mechanism in a managerial register

**Decision Alteration.** Rather than persuading the target to choose differently within fixed criteria, this tactic changes the criteria themselves, so the target's existing capital is silently reclassified as worth less. The decision is altered not by argument but by repricing — the asset the target holds (their expertise, their credential, their style of work) is moved from the asset column to a neutral or liability column in the organization's official accounting of merit

**Optimal Conditions.** Works best where the criteria of evaluation are formalized and revisable by a small group (HR competency models, accreditation standards, editorial style guides, regulatory rubrics, ranking methodologies); where the rewriter has institutional authority to issue the new version; where the affected population is large enough that any one objection looks parochial; and where a credible external narrative (digital transformation, scientific rigor, customer-centricity, ESG, AI) lends the rewrite the look of inevitability rather than politics

### Representative Scenario 1

**Context:** Global consulting firm whose new managing partner rewrites the partner-performance scorecard so that 'AI-native client impact' and 'digital P&L delivery' carry the heaviest weights, while 'operational excellence depth' is downgraded to a hygiene factor. The new scorecard is rolled out as the firm's response to client demand, not as a personnel move

**Sample X:** A management consultancy partner who has rebuilt their practice around AI-strategy work is elevated to lead the firm's largest industry vertical

**Sample Y:** A senior partner with thirty years of operational-improvement expertise is graded as 'not future-ready' in the partner review and steered toward early retirement

**Template levers:**

- B(X)↑ ✓ — Partners whose practices map to the new top-weighted categories see their compensation, election prospects, and internal influence climb sharply under the new rubric

- C(X)↓ ✗ — The firm does not subsidize legacy partners' reskilling — building genuine AI-strategy credibility remains a multi-year effort that the partner must self-finance with their own time and risk

- B(Y)↓ ✓ — Thirty years of operational-improvement craft is reclassified as 'commodity work' in the new scorecard, so the same client engagement that would have earned career-defining recognition under the old model now scores in the middle of the pack

- C(Y)↑ ✓ — Staying in the legacy practice means watching younger partners overtake one's compensation, being excluded from the firm's most visible internal initiatives, and being marked in calibration discussions as 'not aligned with the firm's direction'

### Representative Scenario 2

**Context:** A federal agency under new political leadership issues a revised senior-executive qualifications standard that emphasizes 'evidence-based decision-making,' 'data analytics,' and 'evaluation methodology' over 'program operations' and 'stakeholder relationship management.' The new standard is framed as a response to oversight pressure for rigor, not as a purge

**Sample X:** A government agency analyst who has built skill in quantitative impact-evaluation methods is selected for the agency's new senior-grade leadership cohort

**Sample Y:** A senior analyst with deep program-implementation and stakeholder-management experience is rated 'developing' against the new criteria and slowly squeezed out via lateral moves

**Template levers:**

- B(X)↑ ✓ — Quantitatively credentialed staff move quickly into the senior-grade slots and into the new agency offices created around the analytics agenda

- C(X)↓ ✗ — Mid-career operational staff get no protected time, no funded master's-level statistics training, and no waiver from the new qualification — the cost of meeting it falls entirely on them

- B(Y)↓ ✓ — Twenty years of program-implementation knowledge — knowing how to actually deliver a federal grant program — is reframed as a tactical competency rather than strategic leadership material, demoting its value in the qualification standard

- C(Y)↑ ✓ — Career analysts who don't reframe themselves face stuck grades, exclusion from the new political leadership's inner circle, and a real prospect of being put through a reduction-in-force when the next reorganization arrives

### Versatility

**Level:** high

**Explanation.** Operates anywhere evaluation criteria are codified and revisable — corporate competency models, military officer-rating systems, academic tenure standards, accreditation rubrics, editorial style guides, regulatory examination criteria, sports scouting frameworks, and ranking methodologies. Less effective where criteria are externally fixed (legally mandated qualifications, hard scientific benchmarks) or where the population being evaluated has strong external markets that don't recognize the internal rewrite

### Application Examples

- Corporate: A new CHRO rolls out 'leadership principles' that map to the new CEO's preferred profile, and within two annual cycles the leadership team has been quietly turned over without a single dismissal-for-cause
- Academic: An economics department revises tenure criteria to weight top-five-journal publications and credibility-revolution methods, leaving heterodox or qualitative researchers structurally unpromotable
- Military: A service revises its officer evaluation report to weight joint and cyber experience over traditional combat-arms credentials, shifting the promotion pipeline toward officers whose careers were built around the new domains
- Regulatory: A financial regulator rewrites its examination manual to emphasize model-risk and climate-risk capabilities, devaluing the credit-cycle and operational-risk expertise that defined senior examiners for a generation
- Religious: A denomination revises its ordination standards to require new theological and pastoral-care competencies, effectively retiring an older cohort of clergy who built their authority on a prior catechetical model
- Sports: A professional league's general manager replaces traditional scouting categories with sabermetric and biometric measures, ending the careers of veteran scouts whose value rested on the old framework

## Integrated Insights

### Pattern Recognition

The competency-model coup is a clean instance of asset repricing as a political weapon: the legacy cohort is not attacked, not demoted, and not even criticized — their assets are simply revalued in the official ledger. Across the cluster, this sits next to STRATEGIC OBSOLESCENCE (0206) and THE 'FUTURE OBSOLESCENCE' THREAT (0680) but is operationally distinct. Strategic Obsolescence works by changing what the organization does, so the skills become unused. The 'Future Obsolescence' threat works by predicting irrelevance to induce voluntary behavior change. This tactic works on the measurement layer itself — the work doesn't change, the rubric does. It is the most administratively elegant of the three because the destruction of incumbent value is encoded in HR systems and carries the moral authority of 'modernization' rather than the political stain of confrontation

### Strategic Implications

For the architect, the implication is that controlling the definitions of merit is more powerful than winning any particular argument about merit. For the legacy cohort, the implication is that defending one's existing credentials within the new framework is the losing game — the framework itself is the battleground. The only durable counters are (a) capturing or contesting the rewrite process before it ships (challenging which competencies appear and how they're weighted), (b) building a parallel external credential market that the internal rewrite cannot devalue (industry certifications, public reputation, board roles), or (c) repositioning one's own profile fast enough to ride the new rubric rather than being measured against it. Note that this tactic is more dangerous than the Resistance Triple of UNSPOKEN RULES (0007) — that one constrains behavior at the margin, this one rewrites the constitution of value

### Resolution Approach

Use the specific lens when diagnosing a concrete situation — a new leader, a pending HR system rewrite, a specific cohort about to be invalidated — because the practitioner needs to identify the venues where the rewrite is being drafted and the leverage points for influencing it. Use the generic lens when teaching the broader pattern of definition-control as an influence move, because the same logic applies to accreditation bodies, regulators, academic gatekeepers, and ranking publishers far beyond the HR-competency-model setting where the tactic happens to be named

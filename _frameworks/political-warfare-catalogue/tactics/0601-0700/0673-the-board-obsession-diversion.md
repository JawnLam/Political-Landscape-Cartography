# 0673 — THE 'BOARD OBSESSION' DIVERSION

> Focusing the organization's attention on preparing excessively detailed board materials, consuming resources and time to manage appearances rather than addressing operational issues.

**Type:** `hybrid` · **Strategy:** **#13 Y-Focused Triple** · `B(X)↑ + B(Y)↓ + C(Y)↑` · **Cluster:** C67 — Board Whispering & Investor-Layer Politics

**Cited principles:** Goffman

---

## Classification Rationale

**Ambiguity Source.** The title and description anchor the tactic in a specific institutional artifact — the board book — and a specific audience (directors/investors), yet the operating mechanism (channeling finite organizational attention into a high-status performative ritual that crowds out operational problem-solving) is a reusable diversion pattern that recurs wherever any prestigious audience-facing artifact exists.

**Specific Interpretation.** An executive team consumed by board-meeting preparation — endless deck revisions, mock Q&A drills, exhaustive appendices — so that operational issues (a failing product line, a cultural breakdown, a slipping unit) go unaddressed because no senior bandwidth remains to confront them.

**Generic Interpretation.** A diversion pattern in which a high-visibility, high-prestige preparation ritual is allowed (or encouraged) to expand until it absorbs the attention and political cover that would otherwise be available to surface and confront uncomfortable operational realities. Goodhart's Law applied to attention rather than metrics.

**Primary Classification.** generic

**Reasoning.** The board-meeting context is the most legible instance, but the mechanism — using a legitimate, high-status ritual as an attentional sink to crowd out unwelcome problem-solving — recurs in audits, accreditation reviews, investor days, all-hands prep, and earnings calls. The mechanism is reusable across any organization with a periodic external-facing performance, which makes the generic reading more fundamental.

---

## Specific Analysis

### Choice Architecture

- **Option X:** Keep the executive team's attention, calendar, and political cover absorbed in board-deck preparation, rehearsal, and appearance management
- **Option Y:** Reallocate that attention and cover to confronting the actual operational problems — failing units, eroding margins, talent flight, cultural decay
- **Context:** A senior leadership team at a private-equity-backed or public company facing an upcoming board meeting while sitting on unresolved operational issues that, if surfaced, would invite hard questions about leadership competence or strategy

### Active Levers

#### B(X)↑ — Amplify benefit of X — **Active** · intensity 3 (Intense)

Board-prep work is high-visibility, low-risk, and rewards craftsmanship — every late hour spent polishing a slide is legibly virtuous to the CEO, who can point to the meeting as a forcing function. The tactic inflates the perceived strategic importance of presentation quality, so polishing a chart feels like governance, not vanity.

#### C(X)↓ — Reduce cost of X — Not active

The tactic does not make board-prep cheaper — it makes it more expensive and more elaborate. The volume of work IS the point. Cost reduction would defeat the diversion mechanism.

#### B(Y)↓ — Diminish benefit of Y — **Active** · intensity 2 (Moderate)

Operational problem-solving is reframed as 'something we'll get to after the board' or 'not the right forum right now,' subtly demoting its perceived value relative to the imminent, audience-facing task. The implicit message is that fire-drill operational work is undignified compared to board-grade strategic narrative.

#### C(Y)↑ — Amplify cost of Y — **Active** · intensity 3 (Intense)

Anyone who tries to redirect attention to operational issues during board-prep season becomes the person 'not helping us get ready' — politically costly because it slows a sacred deliverable directly visible to the chair and lead director. Raising the unaddressed product line in week 3 of deck cycles makes the raiser look out of step with leadership priorities.

### Strategy

**#13 Y-Focused Triple** · `B(X)↑ + B(Y)↓ + C(Y)↑`

**Rationale.** The configuration inflates the prestige and urgency of board-prep (B(X)↑) while making operational confrontation look low-status and politically expensive (B(Y)↓ and C(Y)↑). Crucially, no one bothers reducing the cost of board-prep itself — the cost IS the diversion. The mechanism's signature is that the diverting activity must remain costly to absorb the attention it absorbs.

**Effectiveness factors.** Effective when (a) board members reward presentation polish over operational candor, (b) the CEO's tenure depends on board confidence rather than operating results in the short term, (c) the operational problems are slow-burning rather than acute, and (d) there is no chief-of-staff or independent director pressing for an operations-only review. It fails when an activist director, a sharp lead director, or an audit-committee chair insists on dropping the deck and walking the floor.

## Generic Analysis

### Influence Mechanism

**Psychological Principle.** Goffman's Front-Stage / Back-Stage Distinction combined with the Attentional Scarcity / Bandwidth Tax findings (Mullainathan & Shafir). The tactic exploits the asymmetric prestige between front-stage performance (what the audience sees) and back-stage maintenance (what keeps the operation alive), and weaponizes the finite-attention constraint to crowd out unwelcome back-stage work.

**Decision Alteration.** Reframes the choice between 'invest attention in audience-facing performance' and 'invest attention in unglamorous problem-solving' so that the former becomes the unquestioned default. The decision-maker never explicitly chooses to neglect operations — they simply find no bandwidth left after the legitimate, urgent, high-prestige ritual has consumed it. The result is decision by exhaustion rather than decision by deliberation.

**Optimal Conditions.** Organizations with recurring high-stakes external rituals (board meetings, earnings calls, accreditation visits, regulatory audits, donor presentations), strong audience-evaluation pressure, and operational problems that are visible internally but not yet visible externally. Most potent when the prestige hierarchy puts performance-craft above operating craft.

### Representative Scenario 1

**Context:** A federal department two weeks before a high-profile cabinet committee appearance where the Minister will be cross-examined on funding requests, while internally a critical claims-processing system has been failing intermittently for months

**Sample X:** Investing every spare deputy-minister hour into rehearsing the Treasury Board submission and the Minister's briefing binder

**Sample Y:** Investing those hours into confronting the systemic IT outage backlog that is degrading frontline service delivery

**Template levers:**

- B(X)↑ ✓ — Briefing-binder polish reads as loyalty and competence; every flawless rehearsal session compounds the Deputy Minister's reputation with the Minister's office.

- C(X)↓ ✗ — Not active — the elaborate prep is the diversion; making it cheaper would free attention the tactic needs to consume.

- B(Y)↓ ✓ — Operational IT remediation is framed as 'a CIO problem, not a DM problem,' diminishing its perceived strategic value to the executive layer.

- C(Y)↑ ✓ — Anyone tabling the IT crisis during prep season is seen as derailing the Minister's appearance — career-limiting in a department where political loyalty is the dominant promotion currency.

### Representative Scenario 2

**Context:** A mid-tier private research university in the year before a regional accreditor's site visit, where the senior team is treating the visit as existential while two academic units quietly bleed students and the hospital affiliate posts widening losses

**Sample X:** Faculty and provost's office attention absorbed in the decennial accreditation self-study and site-visit choreography

**Sample Y:** Faculty and provost's office attention directed at the chronic enrollment decline in two undergraduate colleges and the deteriorating finances of a teaching hospital affiliate

**Template levers:**

- B(X)↑ ✓ — Accreditation prep is framed as protecting Title IV eligibility, which makes every polish-the-narrative meeting feel mission-critical and risk-mitigating.

- C(X)↓ ✗ — Not active — the size of the self-study workload is itself the absorptive ritual.

- B(Y)↓ ✓ — Enrollment work is reframed as 'admissions' work, not provost-level work, which downgrades its status in the senior calendar.

- C(Y)↑ ✓ — Raising the hospital-affiliate losses during accreditation season makes the raiser look like they don't understand 'what's at stake,' inviting peer correction from the president's office.

### Versatility

**Level:** high

**Explanation.** The pattern applies to any organization with a recurring, externally-visible, high-prestige ritual that absorbs senior attention — public-company earnings prep, federal/state legislative testimony prep, accreditation cycles, donor galas in nonprofits, papal audiences in religious orders, IPO roadshows, regulator site visits. Less effective in organizations without such rituals (early-stage startups, owner-operated private companies with no outside capital) or where an external party (activist investor, regulator, audit committee chair) has standing to demand operations-first reviews.

### Application Examples

- Corporate: A public-company CFO running back-to-back earnings-prep dry runs while the FP&A team's warning about a deteriorating segment goes unread in the data room.
- Government: A state agency director devoting six weeks to legislative testimony rehearsal during budget hearings while a known caseworker-staffing crisis in field offices continues to escalate.
- Academic: A university president consumed by trustee-retreat preparation while the dean of medicine's repeated memos about clinical-faculty turnover stall in the chief-of-staff's queue.
- NGO: An international relief organization's executive director absorbed in donor-board pitch decks during a quarterly funding cycle while a country-office safeguarding complaint sits in HR escalation purgatory.
- Religious: A diocesan chancery focused on the optics of an upcoming bishop's pastoral visit while substantive complaints about a problem parish are deferred to 'after the visit.'
- Family business: A founder-CEO of a fourth-generation firm preoccupied with the family council's annual offsite presentation while a long-tenured plant manager's competence concerns remain undiscussed.

## Integrated Insights

### Pattern Recognition

Both readings expose the same operational signature: a legitimate, high-status ritual is allowed to expand without internal challenge until it consumes the attentional surplus that would otherwise be available for confronting operational reality. The tactic's elegance lies in its plausible deniability — no one is consciously suppressing operational work; everyone is just 'getting ready for the board.' Within the cluster, this differs from BOARD WHISPERING (which manipulates a specific director's perception in private) and PERCEPTION GAP ENGINEERING (which actively engineers a delta between internal and external truth): the Board Obsession Diversion does not require active deception, only the passive collusion of a prestige hierarchy that values audience-facing craft over operating candor.

### Strategic Implications

Practitioners who deploy this tactic — whether deliberately or as a drift — buy short-term board confidence at the price of compounding operational decay. The asymmetry is dangerous: the board sees ever-better materials while the underlying business gets worse, and the gap between the front-stage narrative and back-stage reality widens until something external (a missed quarter, a regulatory inquiry, an activist letter) forces the back-stage into view, typically at the worst possible moment. From the target's seat — a director, an LP, a regulator, a senior executive who suspects something is off — the diagnostic question is not 'is the deck good?' but 'how much of the senior team's quarterly bandwidth went into producing this deck, and what did it crowd out?'

### Resolution Approach

Use the specific lens when coaching a senior executive who is being drawn into a board-prep death-march and needs to recognize the pattern in real time — the choice architecture is the actionable frame. Use the generic lens when diagnosing organizational dysfunction across a portfolio (PE firm, foundation, parent ministry) where multiple operating units may be running variants of the same diversion under different prestige rituals — the underlying Goffman / attentional-scarcity mechanism is the actionable frame. The counter is not to skip the ritual but to insert an unrelated operations-only forcing function (audit-committee floor walks, board operating reviews on alternating cycles, independent-director site visits) that cannot be absorbed by deck work.

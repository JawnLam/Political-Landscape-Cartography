# 0318 — THE INSIDER THREAT NARRATIVE

> Using security or compliance concerns as a pretext to implement strict information controls and surveillance that conveniently monitor and restrict the activities of political opponents.

**Type:** `hybrid` · **Strategy:** **#14 Resistance Triple** · `C(X)↓ + B(Y)↓ + C(Y)↑` · **Cluster:** C17 — Crisis Manufacturing, Urgency & External-Enemy Narratives

**Cited principles:** Foucault

---

## Classification Rationale

**Ambiguity Source.** The tactic anchors in a specific organizational machinery — the security/compliance function and its surveillance toolkit — but the underlying move is the abstract maneuver of 'securitizing' political opposition: re-categorizing a dispute as an existential threat so that exceptional measures become defensible. The title points at one apparatus; the mechanism is reusable wherever a legitimate protective function can be quietly aimed at internal rivals.

**Specific Interpretation.** Leadership weaponizes the internal security/compliance/IT-risk apparatus — DLP monitoring, access reviews, communication audits, mandatory disclosures — under the legitimate banner of insider threat protection, but calibrates the targeting, intensity, and follow-through to land disproportionately on identified political opponents and dissenters.

**Generic Interpretation.** The abstract pattern is pretextual securitization: dressing a power move in the uniform of a protective function so that the costs of resistance shift from political to legal/compliance/career terrain, where opponents have less standing to fight back and bystanders feel unsafe defending them.

**Primary Classification.** generic

**Reasoning.** The internal-security framing is the most recognizable modern corporate instance, but the move — laundering a control agenda through a protective mandate — predates corporate IT by centuries (loyalty oaths, heresy investigations, sedition statutes). The defining feature is the pretext-protection coupling, not any particular surveillance technology, which is why the generic reading carries the explanatory weight.

## Specific Analysis

### Choice Architecture

- **Option X:** Conform visibly to leadership's preferred direction, stay below the surveillance threshold, and avoid the kind of cross-functional dissent or external contact that triggers an 'insider risk' review
- **Option Y:** Continue organizing opposition, sharing concerns laterally, escalating to outside parties (auditors, regulators, board members, press), or otherwise exercising the channels of dissent that now read as 'risk indicators'
- **Context:** An organization in the middle of a contested change — a controversial transformation, a CEO under pressure, a major layoff cycle, or a regulatory exposure — where leadership has discretion over how the security function defines and pursues 'insider threat' cases

### Active Levers

#### B(X)↑ — Amplify benefit of X — Not active

The tactic does not argue that conforming to leadership is a better direction; there is no benefit story attached to X. The whole point is that X becomes the path of low risk by virtue of Y becoming dangerous, not because anyone made the case for X on its merits. Naming benefits would defeat the purpose by inviting a comparative debate the leadership prefers to avoid.

#### C(X)↓ — Reduce cost of X — **Active** · intensity 2 (Moderate)

Conformity becomes the default-safe posture: stay off the audit trail, don't cc external counsel, don't message a journalist friend, and the security apparatus has no reason to notice you. The cost of compliance is reduced to 'just behave normally,' which is psychologically cheap. This is a passive facilitation — the tactic doesn't make X actively easier so much as it makes X the un-surveilled state.

#### B(Y)↓ — Diminish benefit of Y — **Active** · intensity 2 (Moderate)

The framing degrades the perceived legitimacy of opposition itself. A whistleblower's concern, once a matter of professional ethics, now gets recoded as 'suspicious external communication pattern' or 'unusual data-egress signature.' The benefit of speaking up — moral standing, credibility, the chance of being heard — is diluted because the act now reads as evidence of risk rather than principle.

#### C(Y)↑ — Amplify cost of Y — **Active** · intensity 3 (Intense)

This is the operating core. Dissent now attracts forensic-grade attention: device imaging, message review, badge-swipe analysis, mandatory interviews with corporate security. Even when nothing is found, the process itself is career-destroying — colleagues distance themselves, references dry up, the target lives under suspicion. The 'cost' isn't a metaphor; it's a real investigative apparatus pointed at the opponent's life.

### Strategy

**#14 Resistance Triple** · `C(X)↓ + B(Y)↓ + C(Y)↑`

**Rationale.** The tactic wears opponents down without ever having to argue the merits of leadership's direction. By making conformity the un-watched state, degrading the legitimacy of dissent through recoding it as 'risk,' and pointing a real investigative apparatus at anyone who persists, it produces compliance through attrition. Notably absent is B(X)↑ — leadership does not need to sell the change; they only need to make resistance unsurvivable.

**Effectiveness factors.** Works best when the security function reports up to political stakeholders (CISO to General Counsel, both to CEO) without independent oversight; when the legitimate insider threat program is mature enough that singling out opponents looks like normal operations; when the surveillance tools are technically real (not theater); and when employees lack legal protection (no union, weak whistleblower statutes, at-will employment). Fails when an audit committee, regulator, or court eventually pulls the case files and the asymmetric targeting becomes visible — the pretext is the tactic's strength and its singular liability.

## Generic Analysis

### Influence Mechanism

**Psychological Principle.** Securitization Theory (Buzan, Wæver & de Wilde, Copenhagen School) combined with Foucault's analysis of disciplinary surveillance. Securitization names the speech-act move by which an ordinary political dispute is reframed as an existential threat justifying exceptional measures outside normal democratic/procedural constraints; Foucault explains why the mere known possibility of surveillance produces self-policing even when no one is actually watching.

**Decision Alteration.** The target no longer evaluates the political dispute on its merits. They evaluate it as a personal-survival question — am I willing to be investigated, surveilled, possibly criminalized, for this position? Because the threat-frame has shifted the venue from politics to security, the costs of resistance are no longer paid in influence and relationships but in legal exposure, livelihood, and reputation. Most people downward-revise their willingness to dissent without ever consciously abandoning their position.

**Optimal Conditions.** A genuine background threat that lends face-validity to the security frame (real insider incidents, real adversaries, real regulatory pressure); existing surveillance infrastructure that can be repurposed without new approvals; weak or politically-captured oversight of the security function; and a population that already views the protective function as legitimate. Hardest to deploy in environments with strong independent oversight (regulated industries with active inspectors, journalism with source protection, academia with tenure).

### Representative Scenario 1

**Context:** A national-security agency under a new administration where the political leadership directs counterintelligence resources toward identifying 'leakers' — defined operationally to include any employee with unauthorized contact with oversight bodies — under the legitimate banner of protecting classified information

**Sample X:** Civil servants quietly process the administration's priorities and avoid contact with congressional oversight staff or inspectors general

**Sample Y:** Civil servants meet with whistleblower-protection organizations and pass concerns to the IG or to congressional committees through protected channels

**Template levers:**

- B(X)↑ ✗ — The tactic doesn't make the administration's policies more appealing on their merits; it simply makes processing them quietly the safest career posture

- C(X)↓ ✓ — Going along day-to-day keeps an employee outside the counterintelligence sweep — the cost of conformity is reduced to invisibility

- B(Y)↓ ✓ — Lawful whistleblowing — once a celebrated civic act — gets relabeled 'unauthorized disclosure' or 'leaking,' eroding the moral credit the act would normally carry

- C(Y)↑ ✓ — The counterintelligence apparatus — polygraphs, communication audits, clearance reviews, criminal referrals — points at the dissenter, making lawful disclosure functionally indistinguishable from espionage in terms of personal cost

### Representative Scenario 2

**Context:** A late-medieval bishopric where the diocesan inquisition — instituted to investigate genuine heresy — is selectively directed at clergy who have been critical of episcopal policy or sympathetic to reform currents, with 'suspect contacts' and 'irregular writings' as the operational criteria

**Sample X:** Cathedral chapter members stop corresponding with reform-minded clergy in other dioceses and confine their concerns to private confession

**Sample Y:** They continue exchanging letters, hosting visiting scholars, and circulating critical treatises about doctrinal abuses

**Template levers:**

- B(X)↑ ✗ — Submission to the bishop's authority isn't sweetened — no new privileges, no doctrinal arguments — it simply stops drawing inquisitorial attention

- C(X)↓ ✓ — Withdrawing into routine clerical work and silence keeps a priest outside the visitation rolls and the censor's reading list

- B(Y)↓ ✓ — Conscientious dissent is reclassified as 'consorting with suspect persons,' stripping the dissenter of the moral and theological standing they would otherwise enjoy as a faithful critic

- C(Y)↑ ✓ — Continued reformist activity now means visitation, examination under oath, possible suspension from office, and the social ruin that attaches even to those acquitted — the protective apparatus of orthodoxy becomes the engine of political consolidation

### Versatility

**Level:** high

**Explanation.** The tactic generalizes anywhere a legitimate protective function (security, compliance, ethics, orthodoxy, public health, child welfare, counter-corruption) exists with discretionary targeting power. It is especially potent where (a) a real threat gives the function genuine face-validity, (b) the targeting authority sits close to political leadership, and (c) the costs of false-positive targeting fall on the target rather than the institution. Less effective in flat, transparent organizations with strong independent oversight; in environments where the protective function has its own professional identity (e.g., an audit firm with reputational stake in impartiality); and in contexts where the press and courts can force disclosure of targeting decisions.

### Application Examples

- A CEO under activist-investor pressure quietly directs the CISO to add 'concerning external communication patterns' to insider-risk scoring, which captures any executive corresponding with the activist's analysts
- A national government invokes a counterterrorism statute originally aimed at foreign agents to compel NGO disclosure of donors, which then enables targeted tax audits of those donors
- A university president, facing a faculty no-confidence movement, asks the IT office to flag 'inappropriate use of university resources' — operationalized as faculty using their accounts to coordinate organizing meetings
- A pharmaceutical company facing FDA scrutiny directs its compliance team to investigate 'unauthorized regulatory contact' by R&D scientists, deterring the candor that would have surfaced safety concerns internally
- A military command facing internal critique of an unpopular campaign reframes a critical junior officer's research correspondence with civilian academics as a potential operational security violation
- A megachurch board faced with a sexual-misconduct reporting movement institutes a 'doctrinal accountability' review of any staff member maintaining contact with abuse-survivor advocacy groups
- An authoritarian regime expands an 'extremism' designation originally targeting genuine paramilitary threats to cover environmental activists who have begun coordinating with international NGOs

## Integrated Insights

### Pattern Recognition

Both readings converge on Resistance Triple (Strategy 14), and the convergence is itself diagnostic. The tactic operates without ever amplifying the benefits of X — leadership does not have to make the case for itself, only render opposition unsurvivable. This is the signature of a power-holder who has the institutional levers to coerce but lacks the legitimacy to persuade. It distinguishes the tactic from sibling cluster members like RISK POSTURING (which markets a threat to mobilize the organization for some action) and THE 'ARSONIST AS FIREFIGHTER' PLOY (which creates the problem one then solves) — here, the threat is genuine in the abstract, and the manipulation is in the asymmetric targeting of a real apparatus, not in the fabrication of the threat itself.

### Strategic Implications

The hardest counter is institutional, not individual. Once targeted, the individual is largely defenseless — they cannot prove a negative, the surveillance is technically defensible, and the procedural costs accrue regardless of outcome. The structural counters are oversight independence (security function reports to a body other than the politically interested executive), targeting logs that an outside auditor reviews retrospectively for political asymmetry, and statutory whistleblower protections that make targeted retaliation actionable. For practitioners observing the tactic from inside, the early signal is not the surveillance itself — which may be routine — but the discretionary calibration: who gets the 'enhanced review,' which behaviors trigger flags, how cases are escalated. The tactic is exposed not by what the security function does on paper but by who, in practice, it does it to.

### Resolution Approach

Use the specific lens when analyzing a contemporary instance with a defined security/compliance apparatus and identifiable targets — the analysis then names the actual tools, the reporting chain, and the protective rhetoric in play. Use the generic lens when studying the move across historical and cross-domain instances (inquisitions, loyalty boards, anti-leak campaigns, modern insider-risk programs) and the recurring structural conditions that enable it. The generic lens is essential for institutional designers because it points toward the structural fix — separation of the protective function from political authority — which the specific instance, focused on the local injustice, tends to obscure.

## Pretextual Securitization Dynamics

** Doc.** Distinct operational signature worth named treatment: the protective-function pretext is what makes this tactic structurally different from simple SELECTIVE ENFORCEMENT (0025).

**Mechanism Signature.** A legitimate protective mandate (security, compliance, safety) is recruited as the carrier of a political agenda. The pretext is what differentiates the tactic — the surveillance is real, the policy is real, the threat is even real — but the targeting is asymmetric in ways the formal mandate does not justify.

**Escalation Pattern.** (1) Establish a credible insider-risk program with real backing — incidents, regulatory pressure, board sponsorship. (2) Expand the operational definition of 'risk indicator' to include behaviors common among opponents (external communication, lateral organizing, ethics complaints). (3) Calibrate enforcement discretion so flagged opponents receive enhanced review and flagged allies receive routine handling. (4) Let the chilling effect propagate — a few visible cases produce widespread self-censorship without further direct action.

**Counter Measures.** Independent reporting line for the security function; targeting-log audits by an external party with no political stake; mandatory disclosure to the audit committee of asymmetric enforcement statistics; statutory whistleblower protections that survive an internal 'security' finding; cultural norms within the security profession itself that resist political tasking (analogous to inspector general professional identity).

**Exit Criteria.** The tactic collapses when (a) the asymmetric targeting becomes documented and externally visible, typically via litigation discovery, regulator inquiry, or investigative journalism; (b) the security function's professional identity resists the tasking and refers the matter outside the political chain; or (c) a high-status target turns out to have independent protective resources (legal counsel, board allies, press relationships) that turn the surveillance into a scandal for the deployer.

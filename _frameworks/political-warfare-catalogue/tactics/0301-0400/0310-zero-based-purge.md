# 0310 — ZERO-BASED PURGE

> Utilizing Zero-Based Budgeting not primarily for financial discipline, but as a political tool to force resistant departments to re-justify their existence, providing cover for targeted defunding.

**Type:** `hybrid` · **Strategy:** **#14 Resistance Triple** · `C(X)↓ + B(Y)↓ + C(Y)↑` · **Cluster:** C14 — Defunding & Resource Starvation

**Cited principles:** Kahneman, Thaler

---

## Classification Rationale

**Ambiguity Source.** The title names a specific managerial technology (Zero-Based Budgeting) yet the described move — repurposing a legitimate review process to force defenders into a justification posture — is a reusable procedural pattern visible far beyond ZBB itself.

**Specific Interpretation.** Reads as a CFO or transformation leader announcing a ZBB exercise across the enterprise with the public rationale of financial discipline, while privately using the universal re-justification requirement to isolate and defund departments that have resisted strategic direction.

**Generic Interpretation.** Reads as the deliberate invocation of a neutral, broadly-applicable review procedure to dissolve the protective baseline that historically shielded a target — converting a defended position into a contested one without ever appearing to attack it.

**Primary Classification.** generic

**Reasoning.** ZBB is the most visible operating dress for the move, but the underlying mechanism — using a universal procedural reset to flip the burden of proof onto incumbents — recurs across audits, charter reviews, sunset clauses, accreditation cycles, and regulatory re-authorizations. The procedural-cover-for-targeted-purge pattern is what makes the tactic dangerous; the ZBB instance is merely its most articulated corporate form.

---

## Specific Analysis

### Choice Architecture

- **Option X:** The resistant department's budget is cut or its scope eliminated under the ZBB exercise, presented as the dispassionate outcome of failing to justify spend from a zero baseline.
- **Option Y:** The resistant department retains its budget at or near historical levels, with the ZBB exercise either softened, scoped around it, or producing a finding that confirms its existing footprint.
- **Context:** An enterprise-wide ZBB initiative announced by the CFO or COO, with a department head (e.g., a long-tenured Chief Strategy Officer or a legacy Innovation group) who has publicly resisted the transformation agenda required to submit a from-scratch justification of every headcount, vendor contract, and program.

### Active Levers

#### B(X)↑ — Amplify benefit of X — Not active

The tactic does not make the defunding outcome itself more attractive — no story is told about new capabilities funded with the recovered dollars at the moment of the cut. The benefit of the purge accrues later and is not foregrounded; bolting on a positive vision would expose the political intent and weaken the procedural-neutrality cover the tactic depends on.

#### C(X)↓ — Reduce cost of X — **Active** · intensity 3 (Intense)

This is the operating core. ZBB provides political cover that radically lowers the cost — reputational, relational, and legitimacy — of cutting the targeted department. The CFO is not 'attacking' anyone; the methodology produced a finding. Resistance to that finding can be reframed as resistance to financial discipline, not as a defensible policy disagreement, which transfers the moral cost of the decision from the actor to the process.

#### B(Y)↓ — Diminish benefit of Y — **Active** · intensity 3 (Intense)

The procedural reset dissolves the historical-baseline endowment that previously protected the department's budget. What used to be a defended position ('we have always been funded at this level for these reasons') becomes a contested one ('justify each dollar from zero'). The accumulated political and institutional capital that made the status quo benefit feel inherent is stripped away in a single procedural move.

#### C(Y)↑ — Amplify cost of Y — **Active** · intensity 2 (Moderate)

Defending the status quo footprint now requires producing detailed, line-item justifications under hostile scrutiny on a compressed timeline, often without analytical support the actor has reserved for friendlier departments. The cost of the defense is real but secondary to the benefit-erosion lever — the department might still win the line-item fight; what kills it is that it has to fight at all from a zero baseline.

### Strategy

**#14 Resistance Triple** · `C(X)↓ + B(Y)↓ + C(Y)↑`

**Rationale.** The configuration is Resistance Triple because the actor deliberately omits direct promotion of the purge outcome — explicitly selling 'why we should cut this department' would expose intent and forfeit procedural cover. Instead, the move quietly facilitates the cut (low C(X)), dissolves the endowed protection of the existing footprint (low B(Y)), and loads administrative burden onto the defender (high C(Y)). The result reads as a methodology producing a finding, not as a leader exercising political will, which is exactly the asymmetry the tactic exploits.

**Effectiveness factors.** Effectiveness depends on: (1) the actor controlling the rules of the ZBB exercise — scope, evidentiary standards, timeline, and decision rights — so the methodology can be tuned against the target while appearing universal; (2) the target lacking strong board or peer allies who can puncture the procedural framing; (3) the cover narrative of 'financial discipline' having external credibility (active investor pressure, recent earnings miss, post-merger integration); (4) at least one or two non-targeted departments emerging from the exercise intact, so the universality of the process is not visibly fraudulent. The tactic fails when the targeting becomes visible — when every survivor is a political ally and every cut is a known opponent — at which point the legitimacy that gave the tactic its power inverts and discredits the actor.

## Generic Analysis

### Influence Mechanism

**Psychological Principle.** Endowment Effect inversion (Kahneman, Knetsch & Thaler, 1990) combined with Procedural Justice legitimacy (Tyler, 1990). Loss aversion normally protects incumbents — the existing footprint is endowed and feels like a baseline rather than a choice. Forcing a from-zero re-justification dissolves the endowment by procedural fiat, converting the protected baseline back into one option among many. The procedural-justice frame supplies legitimacy: targets accept adverse outcomes from a process they perceive as neutral that they would resist if delivered by direct command.

**Decision Alteration.** By replacing 'why should we cut this?' with 'why should we fund this?', the tactic shifts the burden of proof from the actor onto the defender. The default flips. What used to require an affirmative case for change now requires an affirmative case for continuity, against a backdrop where silence equals elimination. This single procedural inversion does more work than any direct argument the actor could make, because it operates on the choice architecture rather than on the merits of either option.

**Optimal Conditions.** Most effective when the actor controls or strongly influences the procedural design (scope, timeline, evidentiary standards, decision rights); when there exists a defensible neutral framing for the review (financial discipline, regulatory compliance, strategic alignment); when the target depends on legitimacy more than on coercion to defend its position; and when sufficient organizational stress (investor pressure, crisis, succession) makes 'universal review' read as prudent rather than punitive.

### Representative Scenario 1

**Context:** A newly hired CEO at a mature industrial firm faces a Chief Scientist whose Bell-Labs-style central R&D group has been funded at roughly constant real-dollar levels for fifteen years on the strength of past breakthroughs. Direct cuts would trigger board questions and scientist exits. The CEO commissions a 'strategic capability review' requiring every research program to justify its budget against the firm's three-year revenue priorities, from a zero baseline.

**Sample X:** Defund and dissolve a long-standing corporate R&D lab whose leadership has resisted the new CEO's commercialization agenda.

**Sample Y:** Preserve the lab's existing budget and staffing under the protection of its historical scientific reputation and prior board commitments.

**Template levers:**

- B(X)↑ ✗ — The CEO carefully avoids promoting the lab's dissolution as a positive outcome — doing so would expose the political intent and undermine the neutral framing of the review.

- C(X)↓ ✓ — The 'strategic capability review' methodology absorbs the political cost of the cut; the CEO is implementing a finding, not attacking a scientific institution.

- B(Y)↓ ✓ — The historical scientific reputation of the lab, which previously made its budget feel inherent, is reframed as 'past performance' and stripped of its protective weight under a forward-looking criterion.

- C(Y)↑ ✓ — Each program manager must produce three-year revenue-impact projections on a tight timeline, an evidentiary form the lab has never been required to generate and is structurally bad at producing.

### Representative Scenario 2

**Context:** A newly seated congressional majority faces an independent regulatory agency whose enforcement priorities have angered key donors. Direct abolition is politically expensive — the agency has constituency support and a defensible mission. Instead, the majority quietly attaches a 'sunset review' provision to a must-pass appropriations bill, requiring the agency to re-justify its statutory authority, budget, and program portfolio before a hostile oversight committee within a single legislative session.

**Sample X:** Eliminate or restructure a politically inconvenient federal agency under the framing of a routine reauthorization review.

**Sample Y:** Renew the agency's authorization on its existing charter, mission, and budget envelope.

**Template levers:**

- B(X)↑ ✗ — The majority does not publicly champion the agency's restructuring as an affirmative goal; doing so would clarify the political motive and mobilize the agency's constituency.

- C(X)↓ ✓ — The sunset-review procedure provides legitimating cover — the committee is 'just doing oversight' — that lowers the reputational and electoral cost of an adverse outcome.

- B(Y)↓ ✓ — The agency's historical statutory standing, which previously made its continued existence the default, is dissolved by the review's structural premise that authorization is no longer presumed.

- C(Y)↑ ✓ — The agency must produce voluminous program-by-program justifications under deposition-style questioning on a compressed timeline, consuming leadership attention it would otherwise spend on enforcement.

### Versatility

**Level:** high

**Explanation.** Applies wherever an actor can procedurally invoke a universal re-justification of incumbents under a neutral framing. Common in corporate strategic reviews, government sunset and reauthorization processes, academic department reviews and accreditation, regulatory mandate reviews, non-profit charter renewals, and military force-structure reviews. Less effective in highly informal organizations where no plausible 'neutral' procedural vocabulary exists, and in cultures where re-justification rituals are seen as so common they have lost their burden-shifting power.

### Core Strategy

**#14 Resistance Triple** · `C(X)↓ + B(Y)↓ + C(Y)↑`

### Application Examples

- Corporate: A new CFO announces an enterprise ZBB exercise after a quarterly earnings miss, using the dispassionate methodology to defund the prior regime's pet innovation portfolio.
- Government: A newly elected administration orders a 'regulatory reset' requiring every agency rule older than ten years to be re-justified or sunset, with selective enforcement of the deadline against politically disfavored rules.
- Academic: A new provost commissions a 'program prioritization' exercise that requires every department to defend its size and mission against enrollment data, providing cover to eliminate humanities programs the administration considers low-yield.
- Military: A new defense secretary launches a 'force structure review' demanding every weapons program re-justify its mission against the new strategic doctrine, providing cover to cancel platforms favored by the prior secretary's allies.
- Religious: A new bishop orders a diocesan 'parish viability review' requiring every parish to demonstrate self-sustaining membership and finances, providing cover to close parishes whose pastors have resisted central authority.
- NGO: A new executive director commissions a 'theory-of-change audit' requiring every program to re-justify its alignment with the organization's revised mission, providing cover to defund programs associated with the prior director.

## Integrated Insights

### Pattern Recognition

Both readings reveal the same load-bearing move: the tactic does not argue for cutting the target — it changes the procedural default so that not cutting requires an argument. The endowment that previously protected the incumbent is dissolved by procedural fiat, and a neutral-seeming review absorbs the legitimacy cost of the outcome. Within Cluster C14 (Defunding & Resource Starvation), this distinguishes ZERO-BASED PURGE from siblings like DEATH BY DEFUNDING (slow attrition over many cycles) and THE 'BUDGET DUST' PLOY (cutting unnoticed): ZERO-BASED PURGE is a single discrete event that flips the burden of proof, weaponizing the legitimacy of universal procedure rather than the invisibility of small cuts. It also differs from THE AUSTERITY JUSTIFICATION, which depends on an exogenous shock — ZBB cover is self-initiated and exists independent of external crisis.

### Strategic Implications

The tactic exposes a fundamental asymmetry in organizational defense: incumbents are defended by accumulated endowment effects, not by argument quality. When the endowment can be procedurally dissolved, even well-functioning incumbents become vulnerable, because they have rarely had to produce zero-baseline justifications for their existence and are structurally bad at doing so on demand. For practitioners considering the tactic, this means the timing and scope of the procedural reset is everything — the same finding produced under a credible universal review is decisive, while produced under a transparently targeted review it is inert and discrediting. For potential targets, the implication is that institutional legitimacy resting on historical inertia is brittle in a way that legitimacy resting on continuously-demonstrated forward value is not — building the latter is a defense against this tactic that direct counter-argument cannot provide.

### Resolution Approach

Use the specific lens when diagnosing a concrete ZBB or strategic-review exercise inside an organization — there, the operational details (who controls scope, evidentiary standards, timelines, decision rights) determine effectiveness and reveal intent. Use the generic lens when scanning for the broader procedural-cover pattern across audits, reauthorizations, accreditations, sunset reviews, and capability reviews — the same burden-flipping signature appears in all of them and the same defensive postures (continuously-demonstrated forward value, pre-emptive zero-baseline self-justification, allied procedural design) generalize across contexts. The two lenses converge on strategy 14 (Resistance Triple) because both readings share the diagnostic absence of B(X)↑ — the moment a leader starts affirmatively selling the cut, the procedural cover collapses and the tactic mutates into a more visible, more contested form.

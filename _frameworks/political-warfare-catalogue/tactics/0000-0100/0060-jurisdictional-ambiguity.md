# 0060 — JURISDICTIONAL AMBIGUITY

> Deliberately blurring roles, responsibilities, and decision rights during transition periods to break down established silos and weaken power bases that rely on clear departmental boundaries.

**Type:** `hybrid` · **Strategy:** **#14 Resistance Triple** · `C(X)↓ + B(Y)↓ + C(Y)↑` · **Cluster:** C29 — Reorganization, Role-Redefinition & Sovereignty Bypass

**Cited principles:** Schelling, Coase

---

## Classification Rationale

**Ambiguity Source.** The title and description anchor in a concrete organizational move (deliberately blurring roles during a transition) yet the operative mechanism — using structural undefined-ness to dissolve the basis of incumbent authority — is a general influence pattern visible in geopolitics, regulatory turf wars, and contested civil jurisdictions.

**Specific Interpretation.** Reads as a transformation-period org-design weapon: leadership withholds clear role definitions, reporting lines, and decision rights so that established silo barons cannot invoke their accustomed authority to block change initiatives flowing from the center.

**Generic Interpretation.** Reads as a universal principle: when authority depends on legible boundaries, deliberately keeping those boundaries undefined disempowers incumbents whose position depends on the boundaries being clear, while empowering whoever sits above or outside the ambiguity.

**Primary Classification.** generic

**Reasoning.** Although the description frames the tactic inside corporate transformation, the underlying mechanism — exploiting Coasean transaction-cost dynamics and the destruction of Schelling-point clarity that fiefdoms rely on — is reusable across any system where formal jurisdiction is a power source. The organizational framing is one instance of a broader category, so the generic interpretation is the more fundamental classification.

---

## Specific Analysis

### Choice Architecture

- **Option X:** Comply with directives from the center despite the blurred lines, treating one's own jurisdiction as provisional
- **Option Y:** Defend the pre-transition silo boundaries and insist that established roles and decision rights remain in force
- **Context:** A mid-transformation enterprise where the executive team has announced a reorganization but has deliberately not yet published a final org chart, RACI matrix, or decision-rights document, leaving department heads operating under explicit role uncertainty

### Active Levers

#### B(X)↑ — Amplify benefit of X — Not active

The tactic does not directly sweeten compliance — no new perks, recognition, or autonomy are offered to those who accept the ambiguity. The mechanism works through the unattractiveness of resistance, not the attractiveness of acceptance, which is part of why the tactic feels neutral and 'managerial' rather than coercive.

#### C(X)↓ — Reduce cost of X — **Active** · intensity 3 (Intense)

Without clear boundaries, sub-units find it much easier to simply route around silo barons and execute central directives. There is no defensible 'that's not your authority' pushback available, so day-to-day compliance becomes the path of least resistance for the rank-and-file caught between the old structure and the new.

#### B(Y)↓ — Diminish benefit of Y — **Active** · intensity 3 (Intense)

The power of a silo baron depends on legible boundaries — the value of 'my territory' collapses when the territory has no map. The tactic does not attack the baron's competence or character; it dissolves the institutional fact that gave the role its meaning, so defending the fiefdom yields progressively less return even when nominally successful.

#### C(Y)↑ — Amplify cost of Y — **Active** · intensity 3 (Intense)

Any attempt to defend the old jurisdiction now requires the defender to publicly define it, which invites adjudication by the very executives who are deliberately keeping it undefined. The act of resistance forces the resister into an unwinnable rhetorical position: they must demand the very clarity that the center has chosen to withhold, marking them as obstructionist or 'siloed.'

### Strategy

**#14 Resistance Triple** · `C(X)↓ + B(Y)↓ + C(Y)↑`

**Rationale.** The configuration produces the intended shift without ever explicitly selling the reorganization. Center neither praises compliance nor attacks resisters by name; it simply lets the unmapped terrain do the work, eroding the structural preconditions for fiefdom-defense while making compliance the lowest-friction path.

**Effectiveness factors.** Works best when the ambiguity can be plausibly framed as a temporary feature of transition rather than a deliberate weapon ('we're still finalizing the structure'); when the center has enough credibility to be trusted with the eventual resolution; and when silo barons cannot quickly coordinate to demand clarity together. Fails when ambiguity persists long enough to be recognized as the tactic, when an external regulator or board forces premature publication of decision rights, or when the unmapped period generates a visible operational crisis that hands the barons a 'told-you-so' moment.

## Generic Analysis

### Influence Mechanism

**Psychological Principle.** Coase's transaction-cost theory and Schelling's focal-point coordination — clearly-defined property rights and authority boundaries function as low-friction coordination devices that make defensive action by incumbents cheap and effective; deliberately destroying those focal points raises the cost of every defensive move while leaving aggressive moves from the center untouched.

**Decision Alteration.** Rather than attacking the incumbent's position directly, the tactic dissolves the institutional substrate the position depends on. Decisions that would have been routed through the incumbent under clear rules no longer have an obvious routing path, and the path of least resistance becomes whichever channel the ambiguity-architect prefers. The incumbent is not defeated in a contest; the contest itself is restructured so they no longer occupy a defensible position.

**Optimal Conditions.** Most effective when the system has multiple plausible authority claimants and no constitutional-level mechanism forcing rapid disambiguation; when the architect of the ambiguity controls the eventual resolution process; and when incumbents lack the standing or coordination capacity to compel premature clarity.

### Representative Scenario 1

**Context:** A reform mayor inherits a city government where powerful career directors of long-standing departments have effectively veto power over policy through control of procedural channels. Rather than fire them — politically costly — the mayor establishes a series of overlapping 'task forces,' 'czars,' and 'cross-functional initiatives' whose authority relative to the existing departments is left deliberately unspecified.

**Sample X:** Newly elected mayor's appointees execute initiatives by routing around an entrenched career bureaucracy

**Sample Y:** Career department directors successfully block initiatives by invoking established jurisdictional procedure

**Template levers:**

- B(X)↑ ✗ — The tactic does not directly enhance the appeal of compliance for the career bureaucracy; it operates by dissolving the basis of their resistance rather than rewarding their cooperation.

- C(X)↓ ✓ — Mid-level staff and external stakeholders can now route requests through whichever overlapping authority is most responsive, making cooperation with the mayor's reform agenda the path of least friction for everyone except the directors themselves.

- B(Y)↓ ✓ — Procedural blocking power, which depended on directors being the unambiguous gateway, becomes worthless when the gate has multiple parallel paths. The director's role still exists on paper but no longer delivers the leverage it used to.

- C(Y)↑ ✓ — A director who publicly demands clarity about which body has authority risks being framed as obstructionist or as defending bureaucratic turf against the reformist mandate, raising the political cost of any pushback significantly.

### Representative Scenario 2

**Context:** A geopolitical dispute over a maritime or land border where one party deliberately avoids forcing a final juridical determination of sovereignty, instead conducting fishing patrols, resource extraction, civilian construction, and administrative registrations in the contested zone. The ambiguity itself is the campaign — clarity would force the international community to take a position the aggressor would lose.

**Sample X:** A regional power gains de facto control of a contested zone through unchallenged practical activity

**Sample Y:** The recognized sovereign maintains effective control by enforcing the formal boundary

**Template levers:**

- B(X)↑ ✗ — The tactic does not make accepting the new de facto reality more attractive to the displaced sovereign; it makes asserting the old reality harder and less rewarding.

- C(X)↓ ✓ — Daily activity inside the contested zone proceeds without enforcement friction, allowing the aggressor's nationals to build infrastructure, settle, and form economic interests that further entrench the practical position.

- B(Y)↓ ✓ — The formal sovereign's juridical claim, which depended on the boundary being treated as definite, yields less and less practical return as the gray zone becomes the operative reality on the ground.

- C(Y)↑ ✓ — Enforcing the formal boundary now requires escalation — naval interdiction, removal of settlers, severance of trade — actions whose visible costs and risks were precisely what the boundary's clarity used to deter for free.

### Versatility

**Level:** high

**Explanation.** Applies across any domain where formal jurisdiction is itself a source of power: corporate org structures, federal/state regulatory turf, geopolitical borders, professional licensure boundaries, ecclesiastical authority, intellectual property scope, and platform content moderation. Less effective in systems with constitutional-grade tribunals that can be invoked unilaterally to force disambiguation, and in small organizations where ambiguity cannot be sustained beyond a few weeks of operational reality.

### Application Examples

- Corporate: A new CEO announces a matrix structure with intentionally undefined solid-line vs. dotted-line authorities, allowing the executive office to direct work around regional barons without ever formally stripping them of their titles.
- Government: A presidential administration creates a White House 'czar' whose authority overlaps with cabinet departments, enabling policy execution while leaving the cabinet secretaries unable to invoke clear statutory jurisdiction against the encroachment.
- Geopolitics: A state pursues a 'gray-zone' strategy in a contested maritime region, deploying coast guard and fishing fleets rather than navy vessels so that the rival cannot cleanly invoke armed-conflict thresholds.
- Academic: A university provost establishes interdisciplinary 'institutes' that draw faculty and budget from established departments without specifying whose tenure clock, hiring authority, or curricular sign-off prevails, gradually shifting power toward the institute directors.
- Religious organization: A reformist bishop creates new lay-led ministry teams whose relationship to traditional parish councils is left undefined, allowing the diocese to route programming and resources around resistant parish leadership.
- Regulatory: A new agency is granted overlapping jurisdiction with an existing one in a way that lets the executive branch choose which to invoke in any given case, weakening the older agency's traditional autonomy without ever formally rescinding its statutory authority.

## Integrated Insights

### Pattern Recognition

The tactic is structurally close to STRATEGIC AMBIGUITY (which keeps the future state vague) and STRATEGIC REORGANIZATION (which redraws the chart explicitly), but operates in a distinct register: it neither describes a future nor publishes a new structure — it simply withholds the present definitions long enough for the old definitions to lose force. It is also a relative of SOVEREIGNTY EROSION, but where SOVEREIGNTY EROSION chips at one target's authority gradually, JURISDICTIONAL AMBIGUITY dissolves the whole categorical apparatus that gave any silo baron a stable claim.

### Strategic Implications

Practitioners should recognize that formal clarity is not neutral — it is a resource that benefits whoever currently holds defined positions. Conversely, deliberate undefined-ness is a resource that benefits whoever sits above the ambiguity or controls its eventual resolution. The decision to publish or withhold a RACI, an org chart, a decision-rights document, or a jurisdictional treaty is itself a power move, regardless of what the document eventually says. The optimal moment to publish is the moment when the de facto reality already favors the architect.

### Resolution Approach

Use the specific lens when diagnosing a particular reorganization where role-definition documents have been suspiciously delayed and operational decisions are bypassing nominal authorities. Use the generic lens when teaching the underlying principle — that ambiguity is a campaign, not a transitional inconvenience — and when analyzing analogous dynamics in geopolitics, regulation, and any institutional setting where formal jurisdiction confers power.

## Jurisdictional Ambiguity Dynamics

**Mechanism Signature.** The tactic operates not by moving authority from A to B but by making it temporarily unclear where authority lives, exploiting the fact that incumbents who relied on clarity lose more from ambiguity than the architect of ambiguity does.

**Escalation Pattern.** Phase 1: announce restructuring without specifics. Phase 2: operate during the undefined interval, letting the center's preferred channels become de facto routine. Phase 3: eventually publish formal definitions that ratify the new de facto reality, framing the resolution as 'clarifying' what was already happening.

**Counter Measures.** Incumbents can demand a fixed deadline for publication of decision rights; invoke board-level or external governance forums whose own legitimacy depends on clear jurisdiction; coordinate horizontally with other silo barons to present a unified demand for clarity before the ambiguity period entrenches new routines; or document operational failures during the ambiguous interval to force a 'told-you-so' reckoning.

**Exit Criteria.** The architect exits ambiguity when the de facto reality has run long enough that formalizing it produces less resistance than continuing the undefined state — typically when the original silo barons have departed, accepted reassignment, or lost the standing to credibly demand the old boundaries.

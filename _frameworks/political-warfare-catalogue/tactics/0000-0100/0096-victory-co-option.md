# 0096 — VICTORY CO-OPTION

> Retroactively claiming credit for pre-existing positive trends or independent initiatives, absorbing their success into the narrative of the value creation plan.

**Type:** `hybrid` · **Strategy:** **#13 Y-Focused Triple** · `B(X)↑ + B(Y)↓ + C(Y)↑` · **Cluster:** C86 — Status Erosion, Prestige Drain & Credit Theft

**Cited principles:** Kahneman, Tversky

---

## Classification Rationale

**Ambiguity Source.** The description anchors in a specific organizational context (the value creation plan's narrative) but the underlying mechanism—retroactive credit appropriation, where causally unrelated wins are absorbed into the actor's story—is a fundamental pattern of narrative annexation seen wherever a new agenda needs a track record before it can plausibly have produced one.

**Specific Interpretation.** Reads as a transformation-era PR move: new leadership running a value creation plan claims credit for revenue upticks, retention improvements, or product wins that were already in motion under prior management or arose from autonomous business-unit initiatives, threading them into the plan's quarterly progress narrative.

**Generic Interpretation.** Reads as the general influence pattern of post-hoc causal attribution—any new actor or program selectively annexing pre-existing positive outcomes into its own causal story to manufacture a track record and validate its mandate before genuine results could exist.

**Primary Classification.** generic

**Reasoning.** While the corporate transformation supplies a vivid setting, the operative mechanism—exploiting weak causal traceability and recency bias to attach floating wins to a chosen narrative—reappears identically in political honeymoons, military commands, sports turnarounds, and central-bank communications. The value creation plan is one venue; the credit-annexation move is the reusable instrument.

---

## Specific Analysis

### Choice Architecture

- **Option X:** Accept the value creation plan's progress narrative—that visible wins (margin expansion, customer wins, retention) are products of the new plan—and back its continuation and resource asks
- **Option Y:** Withhold endorsement, attribute the wins to pre-existing trends or independent operator initiatives, and demand a cleaner causal accounting before further investment
- **Context:** A PE-backed or newly-installed leadership team in the first 6–18 months of a value creation plan, where the plan is too young to have produced large outcomes on its own merits but the board, lenders, and rank-and-file require evidence of momentum

### Active Levers

#### B(X)↑ — Amplify benefit of X — **Active** · intensity 3 (Intense)

The plan's apparent benefit is inflated by absorbing wins it did not produce—a sales team's pipeline that was already closing, a pricing optimization a divisional GM had quietly run, a contract renewal driven by customer-side dynamics—so the marginal ROI of supporting the plan looks dramatically higher than it actually is. This is the central operational lever.

#### C(X)↓ — Reduce cost of X — Not active

The tactic does not reduce the actual cost of supporting the plan—employees still face the same workload, reorganization friction, and adaptation tax. Credit appropriation alters perceived returns, not perceived effort, which is part of why it pairs well with separate cost-reduction tactics rather than substituting for them.

#### B(Y)↓ — Diminish benefit of Y — **Active** · intensity 3 (Intense)

Skeptical narratives that point to independent or legacy causes lose informational power once the wins have been publicly bound to the plan in board decks and town halls. The skeptic is no longer offering a clean alternative explanation; they are now disputing visible 'evidence,' which is a worse rhetorical posture.

#### C(Y)↑ — Amplify cost of Y — **Active** · intensity 2 (Moderate)

Challenging the plan's progress now requires picking apart specific wins the leadership has publicly celebrated, which costs political capital, looks petty ("why are you trying to take credit away from a winning team?"), and risks signalling disloyalty to the broader program.

### Strategy

**#13 Y-Focused Triple** · `B(X)↑ + B(Y)↓ + C(Y)↑`

**Rationale.** The configuration produces the desired shift by attacking the causal-attribution layer rather than the outcomes layer—supporters feel they are backing a plan that is empirically working, while dissenters find themselves arguing against visible results rather than against a contested forecast. The asymmetry comes from the fact that disproving a causal claim is harder than asserting one, especially when the claimed causes (a plan) and the observed effects (wins) are temporally adjacent.

**Effectiveness factors.** Works when (a) the plan's interventions are diffuse enough that causal traceability is weak, (b) middle managers and divisional operators are conflict-averse about reclaiming credit, (c) the board's monitoring cadence rewards recent wins over rigorous attribution, and (d) the prior regime's data infrastructure is too thin to support a counter-narrative. Fails when an articulate operator publicly attaches their name to the underlying initiative before the appropriation, or when an outside diligence party (auditor, lender, activist investor) demands attribution decomposition.

## Generic Analysis

### Influence Mechanism

**Psychological Principle.** Causal Attribution & the Post Hoc Ergo Propter Hoc Fallacy (Kahneman's System 1 causal-coherence bias), reinforced by Recency Bias and Tversky/Kahneman's Availability Heuristic—audiences attribute outcomes to whichever cause is most cognitively available and temporally proximate, regardless of actual causal contribution.

**Decision Alteration.** The tactic does not alter the outcomes themselves—it alters the causal story attached to them. Audiences process "event A happened, then event B happened" as "A caused B," especially when A is the salient narrative actor. By inserting the actor's plan or program as the proximate explanation for already-occurring positive events, the tactic recruits System 1's drive for coherent causal stories into the actor's legitimacy account.

**Optimal Conditions.** Most effective in the early window of a new program or regime, when (i) audiences are searching for evidence the new actor is working, (ii) outcomes-in-flight from prior periods are starting to surface, (iii) attribution infrastructure (data lineage, witness operators, prior public claims) is weak, and (iv) the actor controls the primary reporting channels (board decks, press releases, internal town halls).

### Representative Scenario 1

**Context:** A head of state in their first year, presiding over an economic recovery that was already underway when they took office (e.g., a labor market tightening that began six quarters earlier, a commodity-cycle turn outside any policy lever), uses State of the Union and press conferences to attribute the recovery to their freshly-passed legislative package.

**Sample X:** Newly elected national leader's economic program is judged a success and given political room for further reforms

**Sample Y:** Public and opposition recognize that economic recovery is driven by prior administration's policies maturing or by exogenous global conditions, denying the new leader a mandate

**Template levers:**

- B(X)↑ ✓ — The administration's program is credited with employment and GDP figures that pre-date its implementation, making support for the leader's broader agenda appear empirically justified by demonstrated economic competence.

- C(X)↓ ✗ — Supporting the administration still carries the same partisan and policy costs—the appropriation does not lower the actual price of backing the leader's agenda.

- B(Y)↓ ✓ — Opposition narratives that the recovery was inherited or exogenous now have to fight against vivid attribution claims tied to specific bills and rallies, making the counter-narrative feel academic and motivated rather than self-evident.

- C(Y)↑ ✓ — Disputing the attribution requires opposition figures to look as though they are denying good economic news, which the administration's media operation amplifies as 'rooting against America.'

### Representative Scenario 2

**Context:** A professional sports head coach hired into a team whose front office spent three prior seasons drafting and developing a young roster that was always projected to break through in year four. The coach arrives in year four and post-game press conferences emphasize 'culture changes,' 'accountability standards,' and 'the new identity we've built,' attaching the predicted breakout to coaching philosophy rather than roster maturation.

**Sample X:** New head coach is celebrated as a turnaround artist and given roster authority, contract extension, and program-rebuild latitude

**Sample Y:** Front office and beat writers recognize the win streak as the maturation of draft picks and a returning-from-injury core, treating the coach as a beneficiary rather than the cause

**Template levers:**

- B(X)↑ ✓ — Wins that the franchise's player-development pipeline produced get bound to the coach's brand, inflating the perceived value of the coach's methods and justifying expanded authority over personnel decisions.

- C(X)↓ ✗ — The franchise still pays the same coaching salary, gives up the same draft capital, and bears the same opportunity cost—the tactic shifts perceived returns, not real outlays.

- B(Y)↓ ✓ — Skeptics who point to draft pedigree and player health are dismissed as 'analytics nerds' who don't understand intangibles, especially after each televised win reinforces the coach-centric narrative.

- C(Y)↑ ✓ — Front office staff or scouts who try to reclaim credit risk being framed as undermining the head coach during a winning stretch, which is organizationally suicidal in pro sports.

### Versatility

**Level:** high

**Explanation.** Applies anywhere three conditions co-exist: (i) a new actor or program needs a track record fast, (ii) outcomes-in-flight from prior periods or autonomous actors are surfacing during the new actor's tenure, and (iii) the new actor controls the primary reporting channel. Found in corporate transformations, political transitions, military command changes, sports head-coaching arrivals, central-bank governor transitions, nonprofit executive transitions, and academic deanships. Less effective where attribution infrastructure is strong (mature scientific institutions, well-instrumented operations) or where the prior actor remains publicly active enough to contest the claim in real time.

### Application Examples

- A newly hired Fortune 500 CEO claiming a margin expansion that was driven by a multi-year procurement initiative their predecessor launched, presenting it in the first earnings call as proof their cost-discipline thesis is working.
- A government minister taking office during a policy-pipeline that produces visible infrastructure ribbon-cuttings within months of their appointment—projects whose budgets, contracts, and ground-breakings predate them entirely.
- A military commander assigned to a theater just before a campaign tipping point engineered by their predecessor's operational tempo, accepting strategic credit when the breakthrough lands on their watch.
- A foundation executive director joining a grant program in year three of a five-year cohort, claiming the cohort's measurable social outcomes as validation of their leadership style at the next major donor convening.
- A new university dean attributing a record fundraising year to their 'reinvigorated alumni strategy' when the gifts were already in committed-pledge stages under the prior dean.
- A startup CEO brought in by the board mid-funding-round, attributing the closed Series C to their 'investor relationships' when the round was already oversubscribed by the founders' previous outreach.
- An activist investor taking a board seat days before a long-planned divestiture closes, then citing the divestiture's value unlock in subsequent investor letters as evidence of their playbook's effectiveness.

## Integrated Insights

### Pattern Recognition

Victory Co-Option is not about creating value—it's about controlling the causal story for value that already exists. The lever signature is distinctive: it is the rare narrative tactic that operates almost entirely through B(X)↑ by importing exogenous evidence, and through B(Y)↓ and C(Y)↑ only as secondary effects on whoever might dispute the appropriation. It is the credit-side mirror of scapegoating: scapegoating exports blame for bad outcomes to others; victory co-option imports credit for good outcomes from others. Within cluster C86 it is the most aggressive of the credit-theft tactics because it does not merely dilute or poach a rival's individual win—it annexes whole streams of pre-existing organizational momentum into the actor's mandate. Closely paired with HISTORICAL REVISIONISM (which rewrites the past to discredit predecessors) and CREDIT POACHING (which targets individual peers' wins); victory co-option is the wholesale, narrative-program-level version.

### Strategic Implications

The tactic's quiet power is that it works best when the actor genuinely is doing some new things, because then the appropriated wins blend into the actor's real wins and become hard to disaggregate even by sympathetic observers. The risk profile is asymmetric: the upside compounds (each appropriated win reinforces the actor's legitimacy, which makes further appropriations easier) but the downside is sudden—a single named operator with documentation can collapse the entire credit edifice in a board meeting. Practitioners running this tactic should harvest the narrative aggressively in months 1–12 but plan to substitute genuine attributable wins by months 12–24, before independent attribution starts to emerge. Practitioners observing the tactic on someone else should not contest individual appropriations in real time (it looks petty and rarely wins); instead, document attribution privately and wait for either an inevitable performance gap or an exit moment when accurate causal accounting becomes valuable to a successor or a diligence party.

### Resolution Approach

Classify as specific when the analytical question is about communications and stakeholder management within a defined value creation plan or transformation—board narrative, town hall messaging, what to put in the 100-day report. Classify as generic when studying the broader pattern of post-hoc causal attribution across regime transitions, leadership successions, and program launches. The generic lens is the deeper one because it surfaces the cognitive mechanism (System 1 causal coherence) that makes the tactic work regardless of venue; the specific lens is the more actionable one for operators inside a live transformation deciding how aggressively to harvest in-flight wins.

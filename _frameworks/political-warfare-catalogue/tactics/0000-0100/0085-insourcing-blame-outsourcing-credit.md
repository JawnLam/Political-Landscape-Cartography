# 0085 — INSOURCING BLAME / OUTSOURCING CREDIT

> Structuring initiatives so that failures are attributed to internal teams while successes are credited to the transformation leadership or external consultants.

**Type:** `hybrid` · **Strategy:** **#12 Cost-Focused Triple** · `B(X)↑ + C(X)↓ + C(Y)↑` · **Cluster:** C26 — Scapegoating, Blame Architecture, Expendable Allies & Ghost Sponsorship

**Cited principles:** Goffman, Ross

---

## Classification Rationale

**Ambiguity Source.** The title names a specific organizational transformation arrangement (internal teams vs. transformation leadership and external consultants), but the underlying mechanism — an asymmetric attribution channel that routes failure inward and success outward — is a generalizable influence pattern that recurs wherever an actor can engineer the attribution rules of a shared undertaking.

**Specific Interpretation.** Within a transformation program, structural choices about who owns what (steering committee composition, sign-off rights, brand of work product, naming on slides) are arranged so that line teams sit in the path of failure attribution while sponsors and consultants sit in the path of success attribution. The choice point is whether other senior stakeholders endorse continuing the transformation under this leadership/consulting configuration.

**Generic Interpretation.** An actor engineers the attribution architecture of a joint undertaking so that negative outcomes get coded as 'execution failures' belonging to a delivery party while positive outcomes get coded as 'strategic vision' belonging to a sponsor party. This is the bilateral, structural form of self-serving attribution bias — institutionalized rather than merely psychological.

**Primary Classification.** generic

**Reasoning.** The transformation framing is one expressive instance, but the mechanism — pre-wiring outcome attribution channels so that one party is structurally exposed to downside while another is structurally exposed to upside — recurs across politics, military command, professional services, public-private partnerships, and family-owned business successions. The reusable engineering principle is what makes the tactic strategically valuable; the transformation example is contextual decoration.

---

## Specific Analysis

### Choice Architecture

- **Option X:** Senior stakeholders continue to back the transformation under the current leadership-plus-consultancy configuration
- **Option Y:** Senior stakeholders pull endorsement, restructure ownership, or hand the program back to internal leaders with full attribution rights
- **Context:** A multi-year enterprise transformation (digital, operational, or cost-out) where a CEO/COO and a tier-one consultancy have program-level air cover, while functional leaders and line teams execute the actual work and own the delivery risk on paper

### Active Levers

#### B(X)↑ — Amplify benefit of X — **Active** · intensity 3 (Intense)

Successes are deliberately routed to the transformation leadership and consultancy — board updates, press releases, case studies, and internal narrative all credit 'the program' and 'strategic leadership' rather than the line manager who actually shipped the change. Backing the configuration looks like backing a winning team.

#### C(X)↓ — Reduce cost of X — **Active** · intensity 3 (Intense)

Failures are pre-routed to internal teams via the program's accountability design: line leaders are named as work-stream owners, change-readiness owners, or adoption owners, so any miss reads as an internal capability problem rather than a leadership or advisory miscalculation. The political cost of staying behind the current configuration is shifted off the senior backers.

#### B(Y)↓ — Diminish benefit of Y — Not active

The tactic does not directly attack the merits of returning the program to internal ownership; that alternative is left rhetorically untouched. The work is done by quietly making the alternative unnecessary rather than by arguing against it.

#### C(Y)↑ — Amplify cost of Y — **Active** · intensity 3 (Intense)

Pulling endorsement now would force senior stakeholders to publicly accept the residual blame that has accumulated against internal teams, since the attribution channels are already established. Restoring symmetric attribution mid-flight reads as defending underperformers, raising the political cost of changing course.

### Strategy

**#12 Cost-Focused Triple** · `B(X)↑ + C(X)↓ + C(Y)↑`

**Rationale.** The configuration produces the intended shift because senior stakeholders read the program's results through the attribution channels the architecture itself created: visible wins glow on the leadership-consultancy side and visible misses pool on the internal-team side. Continued backing then looks both rewarding (credit by association) and structurally rational (the named owners of any problem are not the leaders being backed). The alternative — re-symmetrizing attribution — would require an explicit political act that the original architecture has made expensive.

**Effectiveness factors.** Effective when (a) program governance documents and reporting templates are controlled by the leadership/consultancy axis from the outset, (b) internal teams lack a counter-channel to the board, (c) consultancy partners have brand credibility that absorbs ambiguous outcomes upward, and (d) the transformation timeline is long enough that early credit and late blame are temporally separated. Fails when internal leaders have direct board access, when an external auditor or independent reviewer renormalizes attribution, or when a high-visibility failure is unambiguous enough that the attribution channel breaks under load.

## Generic Analysis

### Influence Mechanism

**Psychological Principle.** Self-Serving Attribution Bias (Miller & Ross, 1975) institutionalized through Goffman's Impression Management — the well-documented individual tendency to claim credit and disclaim blame is engineered into the structural and informational arrangements of a joint undertaking, so the asymmetry is not contested case-by-case but pre-wired into how outcomes get coded.

**Decision Alteration.** Observers and decision-makers form judgments about the parties to a joint undertaking on the basis of outcome attribution. By controlling the channels through which attribution flows — naming conventions, reporting lines, sign-off rights, narrative framing — the engineering party shifts those judgments without contesting any individual outcome on its merits. The choice to continue backing the favored party becomes the path of least cognitive resistance because the evidence trail itself has been pre-arranged.

**Optimal Conditions.** Most effective in long-duration joint undertakings with opaque internal accountability, where one party controls reporting infrastructure and another party executes but cannot directly address senior stakeholders. Requires sufficient time and outcome volume for the attribution pattern to compound; weaker in short undertakings or where outcomes are externally adjudicated.

### Representative Scenario 1

**Context:** A Whitehall-style modernization where a consultancy holds the program management office, civil servants execute, and ministerial briefings credit consultant 'strategic frameworks' while attributing missed timelines to civil service 'change readiness gaps'

**Sample X:** Senior bureaucrats and elected officials continue to commission and renew contracts with a tier-one consultancy on a multi-year government modernization program

**Sample Y:** Officials terminate or restructure consultancy involvement and restore programme ownership to the civil service

**Template levers:**

- B(X)↑ ✓ — Consultancy frameworks and decks are the artefacts referenced in ministerial briefings on what is going well, so backing the configuration aligns ministers with the visible 'reform brand' rather than with the unglamorous civil service machinery

- C(X)↓ ✓ — When a milestone slips, the briefing language is 'capability and adoption challenges within the department' rather than 'design or sequencing errors by the advisor,' so ministers are not seen as having backed an underperformer

- B(Y)↓ ✗ — The tactic does not argue against civil service ownership directly; it simply makes the case for it never crystallize

- C(Y)↑ ✓ — Terminating consultancy involvement late in the program now means publicly inheriting the accumulated 'departmental capability' narrative as a ministerial problem, which is politically heavier than continuing the existing arrangement

### Representative Scenario 2

**Context:** A second-generation family-owned manufacturer in slow decline, where an outside CEO is positioned as the architect of 'modernization' and family-member general managers are positioned as the owners of legacy plant performance, so press coverage and bank presentations credit the CEO for strategy and attribute weak results to 'inherited operational debt' carried by family GMs

**Sample X:** A founding family continues to delegate operational running of the family firm to a non-family CEO recruited as a 'professionalizer'

**Sample Y:** The family removes the CEO and returns operations to family members or installs a different external executive

**Template levers:**

- B(X)↑ ✓ — External press, banker decks, and industry awards build a personal brand around the outside CEO that reflects positively on the family for hiring them, making continued backing socially rewarding

- C(X)↓ ✓ — Underperforming quarters are framed as 'turning the ship' work blocked by inherited plant inefficiencies under family GMs, so the family is not held to account for the CEO's tenure

- B(Y)↓ ✗ — Returning to family operational control is not directly disparaged in the tactic — it is simply left looking like a return to the very baseline now coded as the source of all problems

- C(Y)↑ ✓ — Removing the CEO means publicly absorbing the 'legacy problems' narrative that the attribution architecture has spent quarters building, which is heavier than tolerating mediocre results under the current configuration

### Versatility

**Level:** high

**Explanation.** Applicable wherever a joint undertaking has at least two distinguishable participating parties, a long enough time horizon for outcome attribution to compound, and an attribution channel controllable by one of the parties (governance documents, reporting lines, official narrative, press relations). Travels naturally to corporate transformation, public-private partnerships, government-consultant relationships, military command structures where field officers and staff officers share outcomes, joint ventures, agency-client relationships in advertising and PR, family businesses with non-family executives, NGO field operations with headquarters oversight, and academic principal-investigator/postdoc relationships. Less effective in short, transactional undertakings, in domains with strong independent adjudication of outcomes (sports with scorelines, regulated trading with audit trails), or in cultures with explicit norms against credit-claiming.

### Application Examples

- Government modernization where ministers and a tier-one consultancy share credit for 'reform vision' while civil servants are named as owners of 'capability gaps' for any missed milestone
- Military theatre command where a senior general's headquarters claims operational vision while named field unit commanders are written into after-action reports as owners of tactical shortfalls
- Big-firm professional services engagements in which partner-authored slides are presented to client boards while client-side program managers are documented as owners of any timeline slip
- Family-owned business successions where an external 'professionalizing' CEO is publicly credited with modernization while family general managers are framed as carriers of inherited operational debt
- Joint venture governance where the dominant partner's executives chair steering committees and own the public narrative while operational management seconded from the junior partner is named on internal RACI charts for delivery risk
- NGO field operations where headquarters communications credit programmatic strategy to country directors and donor-facing leadership while field staff and local partners are written into incident reports as owners of compliance and delivery failures
- Academic large-grant labs where the principal investigator's name carries publications and conference talks for successful results while named graduate students or postdocs are recorded as owners of any reproducibility or methodology failures

## Integrated Insights

### Pattern Recognition

Specific and generic analyses converge on the same lever configuration (Strategy 12 — Cost-Focused Triple) because the tactic's signature is the bilateral attribution asymmetry itself rather than any context-dependent operational detail. The specific reading anchors that asymmetry in transformation governance artefacts; the generic reading abstracts it to any joint undertaking with controllable attribution channels.

### Strategic Implications

When to use the specific lens: Use the specific lens when designing or auditing the governance of a transformation program, M&A integration, or other long-duration corporate initiative — the lens points directly at the artefacts to inspect (program charters, RACI charts, board reporting templates, press releases) and the structural counter-measures available (independent program assurance, parallel reporting lines for internal owners, board-level visibility of internal contributors). When to use the generic lens: Use the generic lens when diagnosing a relationship pattern across multiple undertakings — for example, recognizing that a recurring 'inherited problem' framing across successive consultancy engagements, joint ventures, or external-CEO tenures is not coincidence but an attribution architecture; or when teaching the pattern to junior operators so they recognize the shape regardless of dressing.

### Resolution Approach

The tactic's power is that it is bilateral and structural rather than narrative and reactive — it does not argue, after a failure, that someone else is to blame; it pre-arranges, before any outcome exists, who will be credited and who will be blamed when outcomes arrive. By the time results land, the attribution channels are already running, and the only way to symmetrize them is an explicit political act whose cost the architecture itself has been engineered to inflate. Practitioners diagnosing this pattern should focus on the governance and reporting infrastructure of an undertaking before any results appear, because the levers operate on the path the evidence will travel rather than on the evidence itself.

## Analytical Comparison

**Specific Strengths.** The specific reading exposes the concrete artefacts — program charters, RACI matrices, board templates, communications plans — through which the attribution architecture is physically instantiated, and therefore points at the operational levers a practitioner can actually move to install, audit, or dismantle the tactic.

**Generic Strengths.** The generic reading frees the pattern from its transformation-program dressing and lets a practitioner spot the same architecture in joint ventures, public-private partnerships, military command, and family-business contexts, where the artefacts look different but the bilateral credit/blame asymmetry is identical.

**Practitioner Recommendation.** When suspecting this tactic, audit attribution infrastructure before auditing outcomes. Ask: who signs the steering deck, whose logo is on the work product, who is named in the press release versus named in the risk register, who briefs the board directly versus through whom. The pattern is visible in the plumbing before it is visible in the results.

**Near Duplicate Distinction.** Distinct from 0561 (THE 'INSOURCED SCAPEGOAT') — both route failure attribution onto internal parties, but 0561 operates through a single specific hire (a shared-services head or internal role) positioned as the named owner of foreseeable failure, while 0085 operates at the structural-and-bilateral level: it engineers the entire credit/blame topology of a multi-party initiative, with credit flowing outward to leadership/consultants as the symmetric counterpart to blame flowing inward. 0561 is a casting decision; 0085 is an attribution-architecture decision.

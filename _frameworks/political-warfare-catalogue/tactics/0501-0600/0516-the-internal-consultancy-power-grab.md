# 0516 — THE 'INTERNAL CONSULTANCY' POWER GRAB

> Creating an internal consulting group or 'Center of Excellence' that gradually assumes strategic decision-making authority from operational units, centralizing control under the guise of expertise.

**Type:** `hybrid` · **Strategy:** **#14 Resistance Triple** · `C(X)↓ + B(Y)↓ + C(Y)↑` · **Cluster:** C31 — Centralization Disguised as Empowerment & Shared-Services Stranglehold

**Cited principles:** Pfeffer, Foucault

---

## Classification Rationale

**Ambiguity Source.** The title and description name a recognizable organizational form (an internal Center of Excellence or strategy office) while describing a transferable power-acquisition pattern: a credentialed expert body gradually absorbs decision rights from line owners. The ambiguity is whether the analysis should anchor in the CoE artifact (specific) or in the more general 'expertise-credentialed authority capture' mechanism (generic) that recurs across legal, audit, planning, IT, and ethics governance.

**Specific Interpretation.** A leader stands up an internal consultancy/CoE inside a large operating company, positions it as advisory, and over time routes more and more strategic decisions through it — typically during a transformation when operational units are too busy or destabilized to defend their decision rights.

**Generic Interpretation.** Any tactic in which an actor erects an expertise-credentialed staff function that becomes a mandatory waypoint for decisions previously owned by operators — Weber's rational-legal authority being engineered into a power base by manufacturing the legitimacy of the function before consolidating its scope.

**Primary Classification.** generic

**Reasoning.** The CoE framing is the most legible instance, but the underlying mechanism — using claimed expertise as the on-ramp to decision authority — is the durable insight. The same playbook generates internal legal review boards, IT architecture councils, Chief of Staff offices, ethics committees, and central planning bureaus. Treating it as generic surfaces the reusable pattern; the CoE case is then one well-developed example of it.

## Specific Analysis

### Choice Architecture

- **Option X:** Operating units route their strategic decisions through the internal consultancy/CoE for 'review,' 'enablement,' or 'alignment'
- **Option Y:** Operating units retain end-to-end decision authority and only consult the CoE on narrow technical questions when they choose to
- **Context:** A mid-to-large company during or just after a transformation, where a newly stood-up Center of Excellence or Strategy Office reports to a senior sponsor and is positioned as an advisory body to business units that own P&Ls

### Active Levers

#### B(X)↑ — Amplify benefit of X — Not active

Deliberately understated. The tactic's distinctive signature is that the CoE does not aggressively sell its own benefits — overt self-promotion would trigger the immune response reserved for empire-builders and convert a quiet authority capture into a visible turf battle. Any prestige conferred by association with the CoE is incidental, not a lever the tactic actively pulls; the centralization works precisely because the function appears self-effacing.

#### C(X)↓ — Reduce cost of X — **Active** · intensity 3 (Intense)

This is the central operating lever. The CoE absorbs analytical work the line manager would otherwise have to do, provides ready-made templates, and removes the burden of having to justify the decision unaided. The path of least resistance becomes 'let the CoE drive,' particularly in a transformation where every operator is overloaded. Frictionlessness is how the consultancy makes itself indispensable.

#### B(Y)↓ — Diminish benefit of Y — **Active** · intensity 2 (Moderate)

Deciding without the CoE comes to look amateur, off-strategy, or undisciplined as 'best practices' framing accretes around the CoE's involvement. A line manager who goes it alone forgoes the legitimacy stamp the CoE now provides — the benefits of autonomous decision-making get reclassified as parochial or unaligned.

#### C(Y)↑ — Amplify cost of Y — **Active** · intensity 2 (Moderate)

Over time, bypassing the CoE becomes politically costly: audit findings, escalations to the CoE's sponsor, exclusion from talent pipelines that flow through the CoE, and the social cost of being labeled a 'cowboy' who 'didn't engage the experts.' The cost rises gradually — early bypassers pay little; late bypassers pay a lot.

### Strategy

**#14 Resistance Triple** · `C(X)↓ + B(Y)↓ + C(Y)↑`

**Rationale.** The defining signature is that the CoE does not loudly promote itself — that would invite the resistance reserved for empire-builders. Instead it makes consulting it nearly costless (C(X)↓ at intensity 3), quietly delegitimizes solo operation (B(Y)↓), and lets bypass costs accumulate through process and audit channels (C(Y)↑). Direct enhancement of the preferred option (B(X)↑) is deliberately withheld — the tactic's effectiveness depends on appearing as service rather than self-promotion. This is the classic Resistance Triple posture: wear down the alternative, ease the preferred path, without making the actor's hand visible as a sales pitch.

**Effectiveness factors.** Effectiveness depends on (a) a senior sponsor willing to backstop the CoE's encroachments during the formative period when its authority is still negotiable; (b) genuine technical competence in the CoE — credentialed expertise must be visible enough to make 'we know better' plausible; (c) operational units being too busy or politically weakened to defend their decision rights; (d) ambiguous initial mandate that can be quietly expanded through scope creep without triggering a formal authority redesign. Common failure modes: a politically weak sponsor, a CoE staffed with obvious mediocrities, or a powerful business-unit leader who frames the encroachment publicly before the CoE has accumulated legitimacy.

## Generic Analysis

### Influence Mechanism

**Psychological Principle.** Weber's Rational-Legal Authority combined with Pfeffer & Salancik's Resource Dependence Theory — authority is engineered by first manufacturing the legitimacy of an expertise-based role (Weberian credentialing), then making other actors operationally dependent on the resource it controls (analytical capacity, templates, decision frameworks). Foucault's notion of pastoral/disciplinary power is the closest secondary anchor: the expertise body governs through 'helping,' not commanding.

**Decision Alteration.** Targets are not pushed to make a different decision so much as they are routed through a new mandatory waypoint that shapes which decisions are even thinkable. Because the waypoint is framed as service rather than authority, refusing to use it carries no immediate cost — but over time, the templates and reviews encoded in the waypoint structure the option set, and the body that controls the waypoint controls the decisions. The shift is from autonomous decision-making to dependent decision-confirmation.

**Optimal Conditions.** Works best in organizations large enough to support a dedicated expertise function, during periods of complexity, transformation, or regulatory tightening when operators welcome help, and where the sponsoring executive can shield the expertise body during its formative period. Requires that the domain be plausibly specialized enough to justify a dedicated function — works for strategy, data science, security, legal, ethics; harder for general management questions.

### Representative Scenario 1

**Context:** A diversified industrial conglomerate stands up a Group Strategy Office reporting to the CEO during a portfolio rationalization program. The office is initially advisory, but its templates become the lingua franca of board capex submissions

**Sample X:** Business unit heads route major capital allocation decisions through the newly formed Group Strategy Office for 'review and alignment'

**Sample Y:** Business unit heads make capital allocation calls directly, consulting the Strategy Office only when they have a specific question

**Template levers:**

- B(X)↑ ✗ — The Strategy Office does not market its own attractiveness; any prestige effect is incidental. Active self-promotion would expose it as a power play and invite countermobilization by business heads

- C(X)↓ ✓ — The Strategy Office produces the analytical pack, the board narrative, and the sensitivity tables — work the business head would otherwise have to staff and defend personally

- B(Y)↓ ✓ — An unaided submission begins to look thin against peers' Strategy-Office-polished decks; the perceived analytical rigor of solo work declines without the Strategy Office's templates

- C(Y)↑ ✓ — Capex going to the board without Strategy Office review becomes a flag in the CEO's pre-read; the business head has to absorb questions the Strategy Office would have anticipated

### Representative Scenario 2

**Context:** A government-funded teaching hospital establishes an Office of Clinical Excellence under the Chief Medical Officer to 'support' departments with evidence-based practice. Initially advisory, the office gradually becomes the gatekeeper for any protocol change that touches multiple departments — and the definition of 'multi-department impact' steadily expands

**Sample X:** Public hospital department chiefs send all major clinical protocol changes through the central Office of Clinical Excellence

**Sample Y:** Department chiefs change clinical protocols within their departments using their own evidence reviews and clinical judgment

**Template levers:**

- B(X)↑ ✗ — The OCE does not actively advertise its own value-add — its legitimacy is built through quiet involvement in successful protocol changes, not promotional positioning that would expose it as a competing authority to clinical chiefs

- C(X)↓ ✓ — The OCE performs the literature reviews, drafts the protocol language, and runs the change-management consultations — labor a department chief would otherwise have to extract from clinicians already short on time

- B(Y)↓ ✓ — Department-level protocol changes start to look less rigorous than OCE-blessed ones; the prestige of independent clinical judgment erodes against the institutional preference for evidence-graded recommendations

- C(Y)↑ ✓ — Quality-and-safety audits begin to flag protocols that lack OCE review; adverse events involving non-OCE-reviewed protocols generate harder questions in morbidity-and-mortality conferences

### Versatility

**Level:** high

**Explanation.** Applies wherever (a) a domain is plausibly specialized enough to support a dedicated expertise function, (b) the organization is large enough to absorb the overhead, and (c) operational units are willing to trade autonomy for analytical capacity. The pattern recurs in corporate strategy offices, hospital clinical excellence units, university institutional research offices, government central planning bureaus, military doctrine commands, and bank credit-risk centers. Less effective in small organizations where the cost of standing up the function exceeds the power it can capture, and in flat partnerships where partners refuse to acknowledge any expertise above their own.

### Core Strategy

**#14 Resistance Triple** · `C(X)↓ + B(Y)↓ + C(Y)↑`

### Application Examples

- Corporate: A newly created Chief of Staff office at a Fortune 500 absorbs decision rights from divisional COOs by 'helping' with cross-functional initiatives, then becoming the routing layer for those initiatives
- Government: A national finance ministry establishes a Public Investment Management Unit that 'supports' line ministries with project appraisal — and within five years no line ministry can submit a capital project to cabinet without the unit's sign-off
- Academic: A university provost stands up an Office of Institutional Effectiveness that helps schools with accreditation — and gradually becomes the body that defines, measures, and rewards what 'effectiveness' means across the institution
- Military: A defense doctrine command, framed as supporting field commanders with best-practice synthesis, becomes the de facto authority on what tactics are taught, sanctioned, and resourced
- NGO/foundation: A philanthropy stands up a Strategy & Learning team to help program officers design evaluations — and that team becomes the gatekeeper for what counts as an evidence-backed grant
- Religious: A diocese establishes a chancery office of 'pastoral planning' to assist parishes with strategic plans, then makes diocesan-approved plans a precondition for capital fundraising authorization

## Integrated Insights

### Pattern Recognition

Both readings converge on Strategy 14 (Resistance Triple) because the tactic's distinctive signature is what it does NOT do: it does not aggressively sell itself. It enters as service, accumulates legitimacy through ostensibly voluntary engagement, and only consolidates authority once dependence has been established. The mechanism that makes this powerful is that the costs imposed on bypass arrive late — after the function has become embedded — which means resisters cannot point to a single decision moment when the authority transfer happened. Within the C31 cluster, this tactic is the 'expertise variant' of centralization-through-helping: where THE 'SHARED SERVICES' STRANGLEHOLD captures power through operational dependence and THE 'ARTIFICIAL MONOPOLY' (INTERNAL SERVICES) does so through forced procurement, the Internal Consultancy Power Grab captures power through epistemic dependence — controlling not the operational input but the analytical frame.

### Strategic Implications

For practitioners building such a function: invest disproportionately in genuine technical competence and frictionless service in year one — the Resistance Triple only works if C(X)↓ is real, not theatrical. Visible self-promotion (pushing B(X)↑ hard) is the most common failure mode; it triggers immune response and converts a Resistance Triple play into a visible empire-building Full Spectrum that operational units will band against. For practitioners defending against it: the inflection point is not when the CoE is created but when the first 'recommended' template becomes 'required.' Operating units that want to retain decision rights should engage with the CoE's outputs while preserving their ability to deviate, document the bases for deviation, and resist letting the CoE's templates become the only legible format for proposals.

### Resolution Approach

Use the specific lens when analyzing a concrete CoE or internal consultancy inside a particular organization — the strength is the recognizable institutional form and the predictable choke points (mandate language, sponsor relationship, template adoption). Use the generic lens when scanning for the pattern across less obvious incarnations — chief of staff offices, ethics committees, IT architecture councils, central planning bureaus — where the artifact looks different but the Weberian-plus-resource-dependence mechanism is identical.

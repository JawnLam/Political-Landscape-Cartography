# 0744 — THE 'KNOWLEDGE GAP' CREATION

> Deliberately excluding specific individuals from training on new systems or processes, manufacturing a skills gap that can be used to justify their marginalization.

**Type:** `hybrid` · **Strategy:** **#14 Resistance Triple** · `C(X)↓ + B(Y)↓ + C(Y)↑` · **Cluster:** C87 — Skill Obsolescence & Competency Coup

**Cited principles:** Pfeffer, Ross

---

## Classification Rationale

**Ambiguity Source.** The tactic names a concrete operational move — withholding training on new systems from specific named individuals — yet the underlying mechanism (manufacturing competence asymmetry by gating access to learning) is a generalizable pattern that appears anywhere a privileged few control which protégés get reps and which do not.

**Specific Interpretation.** A reading anchored in technology rollouts and process redesigns inside formal organizations: managers control training rosters, vendor seats, and 'super-user' designations, and quietly omit the targeted individuals so that when the new system goes live, those individuals look slow, obsolete, or unfit for the new workflow.

**Generic Interpretation.** A reading as a general influence pattern: actors with control over learning channels engineer a capability gap in targets to legitimize their later exclusion, demotion, or replacement — turning a political preference into an apparently objective performance fact.

**Primary Classification.** generic

**Reasoning.** The 'systems and processes' framing is the most visible modern instance, but the deeper move — manufacturing an evidentiary record of incompetence by controlling access to the means of competence — is the defining feature and shows up in apprenticeships, military command schools, succession planning, academic mentorship, and political appointments. The mechanism is reusable beyond the corporate IT-rollout context, making generic the more fundamental classification.

---

## Specific Analysis

### Choice Architecture

- **Option X:** Decision-makers marginalize, reassign, or terminate the targeted individuals on grounds of demonstrable inability to operate the new systems
- **Option Y:** Decision-makers retain the targeted individuals in their existing roles and invest in catch-up training
- **Context:** A digital transformation, ERP migration, or process redesign inside a formal organization, where access to vendor-led training, certification programs, and 'super-user' cohorts is controlled by a manager or transformation lead who also has informal influence over who is later judged competent in the new operating model

### Active Levers

#### B(X)↑ — Amplify benefit of X — Not active

The tactic does not promote the merits of marginalizing the targets. It operates by manufacturing a performance fact, not by selling a benefit narrative. If anything, the actor stays silent about the desired outcome to preserve the appearance that the exclusion is merit-driven rather than chosen.

#### C(X)↓ — Reduce cost of X — **Active** · intensity 3 (Intense)

This is the operating core. By the time the marginalization decision is made, the political cost of recommending it has been laundered through a months-long paper trail of poor performance reviews, missed certifications, and 'failure to adapt' anecdotes. The decision-maker can point to documented incompetence rather than defend a contested judgment — Goffman's frontstage justification is already pre-assembled.

#### B(Y)↓ — Diminish benefit of Y — **Active** · intensity 3 (Intense)

Retaining the targets is drained of upside because their continued presence now reads as a productivity drag on the new system. The investment in catch-up training looks like throwing good money after bad — their colleagues already cleared the bar; why subsidize laggards?

#### C(Y)↑ — Amplify cost of Y — **Active** · intensity 2 (Moderate)

Defending the targets exposes the defender to the same suspicion of obsolescence by association, and to charges of sentimentalism over performance. The cost is not catastrophic but it is real — championing visible underperformers is a known career-limiting move in transformation environments.

### Strategy

**#14 Resistance Triple** · `C(X)↓ + B(Y)↓ + C(Y)↑`

**Rationale.** The tactic deliberately avoids the enhancement lever — the actor never argues that marginalizing these people is good. Instead it quietly reduces the political cost of doing so (by producing a record of incompetence), drains the benefit of retaining them (they are now an operational liability), and raises the cost of defending them (defenders inherit the obsolescence stigma). This is the 'wear them down without selling X' configuration — bureaucratic erosion rather than open argument.

**Effectiveness factors.** Requires (a) credible control over training access and certification gatekeeping, (b) a transformation narrative that legitimizes the new skill set as essential, (c) plausible deniability about who was scheduled and who was not — usually via 'capacity constraints,' 'budget limits,' or 'pilot cohort criteria,' and (d) enough time to let the gap widen into observable performance evidence. Fails when targets have outside training resources, when an HR or compliance function audits training distribution, or when the new system turns out to be less essential than the narrative claimed.

## Generic Analysis

### Influence Mechanism

**Psychological Principle.** Fundamental Attribution Error (Lee Ross) combined with Pfeffer's analysis of structural control over evaluation criteria. Observers reliably attribute failure to fixed dispositional traits ('she can't keep up') rather than to engineered situational constraints ('she was never given the training'), and the actor controls which situational facts are visible enough to enter the attribution at all.

**Decision Alteration.** The tactic shifts a contested judgment ('should we keep this person?') into an apparently uncontested observation ('this person can't do the work'). It does so by manufacturing the evidence the observer would use to test the judgment, while keeping the manufacturing invisible. The decision-maker is not persuaded — they are presented with a pre-filtered factual environment in which only one inference is reasonable.

**Optimal Conditions.** Works best in environments with (a) rapid skill turnover or technology change that makes 'inability to adapt' a plausible disqualifier, (b) centralized control over training, credentialing, or apprenticeship slots, (c) performance evaluation systems that weight current-state competence heavily over tenure or institutional knowledge, and (d) targets without independent learning networks or sponsors who would supply the missing access.

### Representative Scenario 1

**Context:** A teaching hospital adopts a new robotic surgery platform. The chief, who wants to consolidate cases under a favored protégé, controls the case-assignment board and the vendor training nominations. Within eighteen months, the targeted surgeon's procedure volume on the new platform is documentably zero — a fact that drives credentialing committee decisions

**Sample X:** The hospital's chief of surgery rotates the targeted surgeon off all robotic-assisted procedure cases and quietly omits them from vendor training cohorts when the new platform is introduced

**Sample Y:** The chief includes the surgeon in the standard rotation and ensures equitable access to the vendor's certification curriculum

**Template levers:**

- B(X)↑ ✗ — The chief never makes the affirmative case for sidelining the surgeon — that case would be politically dangerous. The tactic relies on the credentialing committee reaching the conclusion themselves.

- C(X)↓ ✓ — By the time the credentialing question arises, the case-volume record makes denial of robotic privileges look like prudent quality assurance rather than a personnel decision the chief drove.

- B(Y)↓ ✓ — Retaining the surgeon at full privileges reads as a quality-of-care risk to the institution, draining the upside of continuity.

- C(Y)↑ ✓ — Any committee member who advocates for the surgeon now appears to be subordinating patient safety to collegial loyalty — a costly posture in a peer-review setting.

### Representative Scenario 2

**Context:** An ambitious parliamentarian threatens the party leadership's preferred succession order. The leadership controls which MPs are seated on which committee briefings and which delegations attend industry study tours. By the time the major defense vote arrives, the targeted MP is the only one in their caucus visibly unable to speak fluently to the procurement details, making them a poor candidate for the shadow defense brief — and for the leadership track that runs through it

**Sample X:** The party's whip system steers the targeted parliamentarian away from briefings on the upcoming defense procurement bill and excludes them from the cross-party study group

**Sample Y:** The whips include the parliamentarian in the standard briefing distribution and rapporteur rotation

**Template levers:**

- B(X)↑ ✗ — The leadership never argues publicly that the MP should be passed over — doing so would create a martyr. The exclusion must read as natural selection by readiness.

- C(X)↓ ✓ — The leadership can credibly tell the caucus that the targeted MP 'just isn't across the brief,' an observation backed by months of visible underpreparation, removing political risk from the bypass.

- B(Y)↓ ✓ — Promoting the MP into the shadow defense role now looks like a gamble — the party would be advancing someone visibly behind the curve on the defining file.

- C(Y)↑ ✓ — Allies of the MP who push for inclusion now have to argue against the visible competence gap, which makes them look like factional loyalists rather than honest brokers.

### Versatility

**Level:** high

**Explanation.** Applicable wherever (a) a critical body of new knowledge or technique is being introduced, (b) access to learning that knowledge is gatekept by actors with discretion over who attends, and (c) future evaluations weight current command of that knowledge heavily. This covers corporate technology rollouts, medical specialty credentialing, military promotion paths, academic mentorship and fieldwork access, legislative committee staffing, family business succession training, and religious or guild apprenticeship systems. Less effective in environments with strong universal-access training mandates, transparent rostering, or robust external credentialing markets where targets can self-source equivalent learning.

### Application Examples

- Corporate: A transformation director excludes mid-career managers from the SAP S/4HANA key-user training cohort, then cites their post-go-live throughput as evidence for the workforce reduction case to the board
- Government: A permanent secretary routes the targeted policy advisor off the AI regulation working groups, ensuring that when the cross-Whitehall AI brief becomes the priority assignment, only the favored cohort is fluent enough to staff it
- Military: A divisional commander quietly omits a rival officer from the joint-operations and cyber-warfare schools, so that when the next round of brigade commands is staffed, the rival's record shows obsolete qualifications
- Academic: A department chair controls which junior faculty get invited onto large-grant teams and into emerging-methods workshops, manufacturing a publications-and-skills gap that justifies tenure denial
- Family business: A founder grooms one child for the bank relationships, supplier negotiations, and tax planning while keeping the disfavored child on operational tasks — by succession time, only one heir is visibly 'ready'
- Religious/NGO: A senior cleric or executive director rotates favored juniors through the donor-facing committees and international postings while keeping a rival on internal admin, engineering a public-presence gap that the board reads as fitness for leadership

## Integrated Insights

### Pattern Recognition

Both readings expose the same structural move: the tactic operates not by persuading anyone of anything, but by controlling which evidence is allowed to exist. It is a Resistance Triple in both lenses precisely because the actor must NOT activate the enhancement lever — any visible advocacy for marginalizing the targets would expose the manufacturing. The tactic's signature is the missing B(X)↑: success requires that the conclusion appear to emerge from the facts, not from the actor. This places it in the same family as tactic 0233 (Strategic Incompetence Cultivation), but distinguished by directionality — 0233 typically describes targets cultivating their own incompetence to avoid burdensome assignments, while 0744 describes principals engineering incompetence in subordinates to enable removal. The vector reverses; the lever configuration is similar.

### Strategic Implications

Because the operating core is C(X)↓ — laundering the marginalization decision through a manufactured evidentiary record — the tactic's effectiveness depends almost entirely on the invisibility of the gatekeeping. Once a target, ally, or auditor can produce the training roster and ask 'why wasn't she on it?', the entire structure collapses and the actor faces a much worse problem than the one they were trying to manage: an apparent pattern of deliberate professional sabotage. Practitioners deploying this tactic therefore invest heavily in plausible cover stories for each exclusion (budget, capacity, pilot selection criteria), and almost never deploy it against a single visible target — they prefer to exclude clusters where the targeted individual is statistically one of several non-attendees, providing demographic noise. Targets and their defenders, conversely, should treat training rosters as the audit trail: ask early, ask in writing, and ask before the gap becomes observable performance.

### Resolution Approach

Use the specific lens when diagnosing a live transformation or rollout — it forces attention to concrete artifacts (training rosters, certification lists, super-user designations) that are auditable and contestable in real time. Use the generic lens when designing institutional safeguards or analyzing succession patterns historically — it surfaces the deeper attribution-error machinery that lets manufactured situational constraints read as dispositional fact, and points toward the structural remedy: decoupling learning access from the discretion of any single evaluator.

# 0730 — THE 'TROJAN HORSE HIRE'

> Planting an employee in a rival department with the secret mission of gathering intelligence, sowing discord, or subtly sabotaging operations.

**Type:** `hybrid` · **Strategy:** **#13 Y-Focused Triple** · `B(X)↑ + B(Y)↓ + C(Y)↑` · **Cluster:** C79 — Intelligence Gathering & Loyalty Mapping

**Cited principles:** Goffman, Williamson, Akerlof, Sun Tzu

---

## Classification Rationale

**Ambiguity Source.** The 'Trojan Horse Hire' label points to a concrete organizational move — placing a person inside a rival department with a covert mission — but the underlying mechanism (covert insertion of a loyal agent into an opposing coalition) is the classical doctrine of espionage and applies far beyond inter-departmental rivalry.

**Specific Interpretation.** A sponsor in one business unit arranges for an employee loyal to them to be hired into a rival unit, where the plant gathers intelligence on the rival's plans, seeds internal discord, or quietly degrades the rival's deliverables while appearing to be a normal new hire.

**Generic Interpretation.** An actor inserts a covertly-loyal agent inside a target collective so that the agent operates from within trust-protected space — extracting information, shaping internal decisions, or sabotaging — while presenting as a member in good standing of the target group.

**Primary Classification.** generic

**Reasoning.** The inter-departmental framing is a concrete instance of a far older and broader pattern — the use of plants and moles documented from Sun Tzu through Cold War tradecraft to modern competitive intelligence. The hire-into-rival-department case is one expression of the doctrine; the doctrine itself is the reusable artifact, so the generic interpretation is more fundamental.

## Specific Analysis

### Choice Architecture

- **Option X:** The sponsor's department wins resource fights, anticipates rival moves, and watches the rival underperform on key initiatives
- **Option Y:** The rival department operates with full informational privacy and intact internal cohesion, competing on the open merits of its work
- **Context:** Two business units inside the same organization compete for budget, headcount, executive sponsorship, and credit on overlapping mandates — a configuration common in matrixed organizations, post-merger integration, or pre-reorg jockeying

### Active Levers

#### B(X)↑ — Amplify benefit of X — **Active** · intensity 2 (Moderate)

The plant produces a steady asymmetric intelligence flow back to the sponsor — knowledge of the rival's plans, internal disputes, and leadership weaknesses — converting the sponsor's strategic moves from blind to informed and materially raising the value of the sponsor's choices

#### C(X)↓ — Reduce cost of X — Not active

Running a plant is operationally expensive and reputationally radioactive if discovered — the tactic does not lower the cost of the sponsor's preferred path; it actually adds carry costs (the plant's compensation, the meeting cadence to debrief them, the risk premium of exposure)

#### B(Y)↓ — Diminish benefit of Y — **Active** · intensity 2 (Moderate)

Discord-sowing erodes the rival department's internal trust and decision velocity, so even when the rival 'wins' a question its win is less complete — the upside of operating as a rival department goes down because the inside of that department becomes a worse place to work and execute

#### C(Y)↑ — Amplify cost of Y — **Active** · intensity 3 (Intense)

Subtle sabotage — missed deadlines the plant could have flagged, calibration errors the plant introduces, escalations the plant times badly — directly inflates the rival's operating cost: missed milestones, executive scrutiny, blown launches. This is the core operating lever

### Strategy

**#13 Y-Focused Triple** · `B(X)↑ + B(Y)↓ + C(Y)↑`

**Rationale.** The configuration is heavy on framing and rhetoric against the rival (Y) and light on logistics for the sponsor (X). The plant cannot make the sponsor's own work cheaper to execute, but it can amplify the sponsor's situational awareness while simultaneously dragging the rival down on both the benefit and cost axes — a Y-degradation play with a single asymmetric intelligence boost to X.

**Effectiveness factors.** Success depends on plausible recruitment cover (the plant must look like a normal good-faith hire), the rival's hiring rigor (background checks, reference depth, internal screening), the sponsor's operational security in handling intelligence (a leaked debrief burns the plant), and the duration: the longer the plant runs, the higher the probability of behavioral inconsistency surfacing. Most plants fail not through detection but through the plant going native — forming real loyalties to the rival department's people and downgrading the original mission.

## Generic Analysis

### Influence Mechanism

**Psychological Principle.** Williamson's opportunism with guile combined with Akerlof's information asymmetry and Goffman's frame analysis. The plant exploits the target's good-faith assumption that an accepted insider operates inside the same trust frame as everyone else; the asymmetry between the plant's presented frame (loyal member) and operating frame (covert agent) is the entire mechanism. Sun Tzu's 'Use of Spies' (Chapter 13) is the classical treatment.

**Decision Alteration.** The tactic does not directly try to change a target's decision. It changes the informational and operational ground on which the target makes any decision — degrading the privacy of the target's deliberations, degrading the trust environment within the target group, and degrading the integrity of the target's outputs. The target ends up making locally rational decisions on systematically corrupted inputs.

**Optimal Conditions.** Works where the target has open hiring (porous boundary), distributed decision-making (the plant gets exposure to real plans without needing to penetrate to the top), low operational security in internal communications, and high in-group trust default. Less effective in compact teams with high tenure, strong vetting, and a paranoid security culture.

### Representative Scenario 1

**Context:** A scrappy growth-stage competitor recruits a mid-level product manager away from the incumbent — except the PM, on leaving, accepts a quiet retainer to stay one more cycle at the incumbent and steer their team's roadmap into known dead-ends

**Sample X:** A startup obtains advance knowledge of a larger incumbent's product roadmap and pre-empts the incumbent's launch

**Sample Y:** The incumbent's roadmap remains private long enough for them to execute the launch on the surprise advantage they planned for

**Template levers:**

- B(X)↑ ✓ — Roadmap intelligence lets the startup time its launch and messaging to nullify the incumbent's surprise advantage

- C(X)↓ ✗ — Running a paid mole is more expensive than running clean — it adds payroll, legal exposure, and operational hygiene burden

- B(Y)↓ ✓ — Steering the incumbent into dead-end roadmap choices means even successful execution by the incumbent produces a worse product than they could have

- C(Y)↑ ✓ — Engineering cycles spent on the steered dead-ends are sunk cost the incumbent cannot recover when the startup pre-empts

### Representative Scenario 2

**Context:** An intelligence service recruits a mid-career foreign service officer of the target country during their posting abroad, then operates the officer through a 15-year career as they rise to senior policy roles — classical 'long penetration' tradecraft

**Sample X:** A foreign intelligence service maintains visibility into a target government's diplomatic positions and shapes the target's internal debate

**Sample Y:** The target government deliberates with full privacy and presents a unified position at the negotiating table

**Template levers:**

- B(X)↑ ✓ — Continuous insight into the target's red lines, internal coalitions, and timing pressures lets the operating service negotiate from a position of asymmetric awareness

- C(X)↓ ✗ — Long-penetration operations are among the most resource-intensive moves in intelligence — handlers, secure communication, vetting, exfiltration planning

- B(Y)↓ ✓ — Policy debates inside the target become less productive as the operating service quietly amplifies factional splits the officer is positioned to feed

- C(Y)↑ ✓ — The target spends real diplomatic capital on positions whose collapse is already known to its counterpart — every negotiation runs at a structural disadvantage

### Versatility

**Level:** high

**Explanation.** Applies wherever a target group has porous recruitment boundaries, an in-group trust default, and decision processes that distribute access to internally-sensitive information. Documented across military intelligence, competitive corporate intelligence, labor union infiltration, organized crime task forces, religious sect monitoring, political party penetration, and inter-departmental corporate rivalry. Less applicable in tight-knit groups with strong vetting (early-stage founder teams, special operations units, intimate political conspiracies) where the trust default is paranoia rather than openness.

### Application Examples

- Corporate: A competing tech firm recruits a disaffected engineer from a rival's AI team and arranges for them to take a position back at the rival under a six-month retainer to surface the rival's training data sourcing methods
- Government: An intelligence agency runs a long-penetration asset through a target country's foreign ministry for two decades, gaining advance knowledge of every major treaty position
- Activism: An environmental group quietly places a staffer inside a corporate sustainability function to surface internal dissent and time public campaigns to coincide with internal credibility crises
- Lobbying: A trade association funds a 'policy researcher' position inside an opposing think tank, where the placement softens the think tank's published positions on key regulatory questions
- Military: A counterintelligence service places officers inside foreign embassies under diplomatic cover, generating reporting that shapes the host country's negotiating posture for a decade
- Family business: A faction in a multi-generational family enterprise sponsors a 'neutral' professional manager into the rival faction's pet division, where the manager subtly mismanages the division to weaken its political claim on succession
- Religious/NGO: A reform-minded coalition inside a denomination places allies on a traditionalist committee, where they slow the committee's output and surface internal disagreements to a sympathetic press

## Integrated Insights

### Pattern Recognition

The tactic's signature is that it operates entirely inside the trust frame of the target — the plant's effectiveness is exactly proportional to how completely the target treats them as one of their own. This makes it distinct from sibling tactics in the same cluster: 'The Babysitter Assignment' uses official authority to surveil from above; 'Reverse Mentorship Surveillance' uses an asymmetric pairing structure; 'Amnesty Baiting' lures targets into self-disclosure. The Trojan Horse Hire is the only one in the cluster where the surveillance asset is structurally invisible — the target does not know they are under surveillance, which is both the tactic's power and its operational vulnerability (loss of cover is catastrophic and unrecoverable).

### Strategic Implications

The tactic creates a moral hazard that compounds over time. A plant who stays long enough either (a) goes native and quietly fails the mission, or (b) becomes so embedded in the rival's social fabric that 'extracting' them — pulling them back for promotion, reassignment, or testimony — itself becomes a tell. Sophisticated operators run plants on time-boxed missions with planned exfiltration; amateur operators run them indefinitely and discover years later that the plant has spent the last three reporting cycles feeding them sanitized middle-distance information while protecting their adopted department. This is the same dynamic by which intelligence services lose long-running agents to their host services. For the rival, the existence of plants is best assumed rather than detected; the defensive posture is information compartmentation and rotation of access, not witch-hunting.

### Resolution Approach

Use the specific interpretation when analyzing concrete inter-departmental hires in matrix-organization politics or post-merger integration jockeying — the choice architecture, the active levers, and the operational hygiene questions all map cleanly to that bounded scenario. Use the generic interpretation when teaching the tactic as a doctrine, comparing it across domains (corporate / intelligence / activism), or designing defenses — at the doctrinal level, the cross-domain similarities are more useful than the inter-departmental specifics.

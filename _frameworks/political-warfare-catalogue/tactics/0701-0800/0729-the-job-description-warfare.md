# 0729 — THE 'JOB DESCRIPTION' WARFARE

> Rewriting the job descriptions of key roles during a reorganization to subtly expand the influence of allies and diminish the scope of rivals.

**Type:** `hybrid` · **Strategy:** **#14 Resistance Triple** · `C(X)↓ + B(Y)↓ + C(Y)↑` · **Cluster:** C29 — Reorganization, Role-Redefinition & Sovereignty Bypass

**Cited principles:** Goffman, Pfeffer, Foucault

---

## Classification Rationale

**Ambiguity Source.** The title anchors in a specific bureaucratic artifact (the job description, rewritten during reorganization), but the underlying move — covertly redrawing formal scope to shift power between actors — is a general bureaucratic-warfare pattern that recurs anywhere written role definitions confer authority.

**Specific Interpretation.** During a reorganization, the actor controlling the JD-drafting process expands allies' formal remit (decision rights, budget control, headcount, reporting lines) and contracts rivals' remit, dressing each adjustment as routine role clarification that flows from the new structure.

**Generic Interpretation.** An influence pattern where the actor with control over formal-authority documents (charters, terms of reference, RACI matrices, policy language, statutes) rewrites the underlying rules of engagement to advantage chosen parties — letting the document, rather than the actor, do the visible work of power redistribution.

**Primary Classification.** generic

**Reasoning.** The reorganization context is the most legible vehicle, but the underlying mechanism — weaponizing the editorial pen over governing documents — generalizes to constitutions, committee charters, audit scopes, contract scoping, regulatory rulemaking, and standards bodies. The 'JD' is one specimen of a much broader class of formal-authority instruments. Strategy travels; document doesn't.

---

## Specific Analysis

### Choice Architecture

- **Option X:** Accept the rewritten job descriptions as a routine output of the reorganization, signing off without forensic comparison to prior scope
- **Option Y:** Contest individual JD changes line-by-line, demanding justification for each scope adjustment and surfacing the political consequences
- **Context:** A reorganization is in flight; an actor (often a chief of staff, HRBP, or favored deputy) has been granted editorial control over the JD redraft. Affected leaders are absorbing structure-chart changes, new reporting lines, and headcount decisions simultaneously, which crowds out attention to the document layer.

### Active Levers

#### B(X)↑ — Amplify benefit of X — **Active** · intensity 1 (Subtle)

Mildly active and deliberately understated. The JDs do enlarge allies' scope (more benefit), but the tactic's discipline is to avoid 'selling' the changes — selling would expose the play. Benefit accrues to allies without rhetorical amplification, which is part of the signature.

#### C(X)↓ — Reduce cost of X — **Active** · intensity 3 (Intense)

Core operating lever. The rewritten JD becomes the formal warrant that lets allies exercise expanded authority without political cost — they are no longer 'overreaching,' they are 'doing their job.' The document converts what would have been a contested power grab into routine execution of an approved role.

#### B(Y)↓ — Diminish benefit of Y — **Active** · intensity 2 (Moderate)

Rivals' shrunken JDs strip them of decisions they previously owned — those decisions, and the visibility/influence that came with them, are now formally someone else's. The remaining role still functions but yields less power, status, and career optionality.

#### C(Y)↑ — Amplify cost of Y — **Active** · intensity 3 (Intense)

Rivals who try to act on their old scope now incur friction — every cross-boundary move requires negotiation with the ally whose JD now covers that ground. Contesting the document itself is even costlier: it requires admitting the rewrite was political, which sounds like sour grapes against an approved structure.

### Strategy

**#14 Resistance Triple** · `C(X)↓ + B(Y)↓ + C(Y)↑`

**Rationale.** The configuration is Resistance Triple because the tactic deliberately suppresses the B(X)↑ lever — direct promotion of the allies' new power would invite resistance and expose the play. Instead the actor quietly lowers the political cost of allies wielding expanded authority (formal warrant), drains the rival role of its valuable decisions, and raises the cost of rival action by making any cross-boundary move look like insubordination against the new structure.

**Effectiveness factors.** Effectiveness depends on (1) the reorganization providing cover — a standalone JD rewrite outside a reorg is far more transparent and contestable, (2) the actor having editorial control and time pressure on reviewers, (3) the absence of a side-by-side diff process or external HR audit, and (4) the affected leaders' attention being absorbed by larger reorg signals (titles, reporting lines, headcount) so the scope language slides through. It fails when a rival demands a redline comparison, or when a strong HRBP or works council insists on documenting scope deltas explicitly.

## Generic Analysis

### Influence Mechanism

**Psychological Principle.** Pfeffer's theory of organizational power (control over formal authority structures as a power source) combined with Foucault's analysis of disciplinary power (bureaucratic documents as instruments through which power operates impersonally). The bureaucratic register also draws on Goffman's frame analysis — presenting power redistribution under the frame of 'routine administrative housekeeping' rather than 'political contest' changes how observers attend to it.

**Decision Alteration.** The tactic substitutes the actor's contested power claim with the document's uncontested authority. The target no longer faces 'do I let this person have more power?' but rather 'do I overturn the approved role definition?' — a much higher-bar decision, because overturning the document means admitting the larger structure was politically compromised. The framing converts a discretionary political question into a status quo defense, which most observers will not undertake.

**Optimal Conditions.** Works best in organizations that (a) treat formal documents as authoritative, (b) have weak version-control or diff practices on governance artifacts, (c) are in a period of structural change that lowers attention to document-layer detail, and (d) lack independent third-party review of scope changes. Highly bureaucratic organizations are paradoxically the most vulnerable — their respect for the document is the lever.

### Representative Scenario 1

**Context:** A board governance reform at a publicly-traded company gives the Risk Committee chair editorial control over the redrafted committee charters. The Risk charter's revised language quietly absorbs cybersecurity oversight from the Audit Committee, framed as 'clarifying overlapping responsibilities.' Audit Committee members signing off on the package don't catch the scope migration until quarterly reporting cycles surface the new ownership.

**Sample X:** An allied committee chair quietly extends a charter to cover decisions previously held by a rival committee

**Sample Y:** Affected committees and stakeholders contest the charter expansion as a jurisdictional encroachment

**Template levers:**

- B(X)↑ ✓ — Risk chair now formally owns a hot-button area (cyber) that brings board prominence and budget — but the charter language is dry enough that the gain is invisible at sign-off.

- C(X)↓ ✓ — Acting on cyber oversight is now charter-warranted, removing the cost of seeking concurrence from Audit.

- B(Y)↓ ✓ — Audit Committee loses one of its most board-visible domains, reducing the role's profile and the chair's standing.

- C(Y)↑ ✓ — Audit's chair re-asserting cyber oversight now requires contesting an approved charter, which reads as turf war rather than principled governance.

### Representative Scenario 2

**Context:** During an omnibus government reorganization bill, a state legislator chairing a structural review committee oversees the rewrite of multiple agencies' authorizing statutes. Definitional language ('the Department shall have primary jurisdiction over...') is adjusted to migrate environmental permitting authority from a science-led department to a business-friendly economic development agency, presented as a 'streamlining' adjustment within a broader reorganization.

**Sample X:** A state legislator inserts subtle scope language into agency authorizing statutes that quietly transfers regulatory authority to a favored agency

**Sample Y:** The displaced agency or affected industry contests the transfer as a substantive policy change requiring deliberation

**Template levers:**

- B(X)↑ ✓ — The favored agency gains a substantive new authority — but it appears in the bill as a definitional clarification, not as a contested transfer.

- C(X)↓ ✓ — The favored agency now acts under explicit statutory authority, removing the inter-agency negotiation cost that previously governed permitting.

- B(Y)↓ ✓ — The displaced agency's mission is hollowed — its remaining functions are less consequential and harder to justify for funding.

- C(Y)↑ ✓ — The displaced agency or affected industry must now pursue legislative repeal to undo the transfer, a much higher bar than blocking it in initial debate.

### Versatility

**Level:** high

**Explanation.** Operates anywhere formal documents allocate authority — corporate JDs, committee charters, RACI matrices, terms of reference, agency statutes, treaty annexes, standards-body scope statements, NGO bylaws, university faculty handbooks. Less effective in flat, oral-tradition organizations where authority comes from relationships rather than documents, and in organizations with strong diff-review or audit-trail practices on governance artifacts.

### Core Strategy

### Application Examples

- Corporate: A chief of staff drafting reorganization JDs extends the favored VP's purview over 'cross-functional initiatives' — a phrase that quietly captures the rival VP's signature programs.
- Government: A presidential transition team rewriting the National Security Council's terms of reference shifts interagency convening authority from State to the NSC itself, embedded as a procedural clarification.
- Academic: A faculty committee chair rewriting departmental bylaws during a merger of two departments quietly assigns curriculum authority to the unit whose chair is an ally, framed as 'consolidating duplicated governance.'
- Lobbying: An industry association capturing the pen on a regulatory rule's scope definition narrows the rule's applicability to exempt its members, with the narrowing buried in definitional language rather than substantive carve-outs.
- NGO: A coalition's coordinating committee rewriting member responsibilities during a strategic refresh quietly shifts grant-making authority to allied member organizations while assigning logistical 'support' roles to dissenting members.
- Family business: A succession-planning consultant drafting new role definitions for the next generation expands the founder-favored child's 'operational oversight' to include functions previously held by a sibling, with the change framed as natural alignment with the new strategic plan.

## Integrated Insights

### Pattern Recognition

The specific (reorganization JDs) and generic (formal-document warfare) analyses converge on the same Resistance Triple configuration, which is itself diagnostic — this tactic's signature is the deliberate suppression of the B(X)↑ lever. Whenever an actor with power could amplify their allies' benefits but instead routes the entire move through document edits, the absence of the sales pitch is the tell. Compare with the cluster's sibling JURISDICTIONAL AMBIGUITY (which keeps boundaries vague to enable opportunism) and STRATEGIC FRAGMENTATION (which fractures structure to disempower): JD warfare is the opposite — it uses precision in the document to lock in the redistribution before anyone notices.

### Strategic Implications

Practitioners deploying this tactic must accept its operational discipline: any visible promotion of the allies' new power destroys the bureaucratic cover that makes the move work. Targets and defenders should treat any document-rewrite during structural change as an attack surface — redlines, side-by-side diffs, and 'scope delta' summaries are cheap forensics that neutralize the tactic almost completely. The broader implication is that in document-heavy organizations, editorial control over governance artifacts during change windows is a more concentrated power than most formal hierarchies recognize, which is why staff roles like chief of staff, general counsel's office, and HR partner often outpunch their org-chart weight during reorgs.

### Resolution Approach

Classify as generic when teaching the pattern (the document-warfare lens applies across charters, statutes, bylaws, contracts); classify as specific when analyzing a live reorganization where the JD is the visible instrument and the question is which clauses to contest. In live use, the specific lens points to defensive moves (demand a diff, require HRBP sign-off on scope changes, escalate disputed clauses to a neutral reviewer); the generic lens points to recognition — spotting the same play in committee charters, statute drafts, and standards documents where the practitioner might otherwise miss it.

# 0184 — CREDIT POACHING

> The systematic practice of senior leaders or aligned factions taking ownership of successful ideas or outcomes generated by others, often political opponents, to bolster their own position and demoralize the originators.

**Type:** `hybrid` · **Strategy:** **#13 Y-Focused Triple** · `B(X)↑ + B(Y)↓ + C(Y)↑` · **Cluster:** C86 — Status Erosion, Prestige Drain & Credit Theft

**Cited principles:** Goffman, Akerlof

---

## Classification Rationale

**Ambiguity Source.** The description references a specific organizational dynamic ('senior leaders or aligned factions,' 'political opponents,' the 'originators') anchored in factional power struggle, but the underlying mechanism — appropriating attribution of a success to oneself while denying it to the producer — is a fully reusable pattern of impression management and status redistribution that operates wherever attribution is ambiguous.

**Specific Interpretation.** A senior leader or aligned faction in an organizational power struggle systematically claims authorship of successful work generated by political opponents, simultaneously enhancing the senior's standing with superiors and starving the originators of the visibility they need to advance.

**Generic Interpretation.** A general influence pattern in which the actor exploits attribution ambiguity — the gap between who did the work and who is publicly associated with it — to harvest the reputational rewards of others' output while leaving the producers structurally weaker.

**Primary Classification.** generic

**Reasoning.** The factional framing in the description is illustrative rather than essential — the mechanism does not require named political opponents to function. What is essential is (a) an audience that judges by attributed credit rather than direct observation, and (b) a producer whose visibility is mediated by the actor. Wherever those two conditions exist — academic labs, sales teams, government bureaucracies, family businesses — credit poaching operates identically, which makes the generic reading more fundamental.

---

## Specific Analysis

### Choice Architecture

- **Option X:** The senior leader is recognized by the executive audience as the source of the successful idea/outcome and is rewarded accordingly
- **Option Y:** The originator (often a political rival or rival's protégé) retains attribution and accumulates the standing the win would normally confer
- **Context:** An organization where multiple factions compete for executive favor and where the senior leader sits structurally between the originators and the recognizing audience — controlling which name attaches to the work as it travels upward

### Active Levers

#### B(X)↑ — Amplify benefit of X — **Active** · intensity 3 (Intense)

The entire tactic is engineered around boosting the senior's perceived contribution. By attaching their name to the successful outcome — through reframing in upward presentations, executive summaries, or board recaps — the senior accrues the full reputational dividend of a win they did not produce. This is the central operating lever.

#### C(X)↓ — Reduce cost of X — Not active

The tactic does not lower the executive audience's cost of siding with the senior leader — that cost was already low. Credit poaching operates on perceived merit, not on the friction of choosing the senior. The absence here is diagnostic: this is a status play, not a switching play.

#### B(Y)↓ — Diminish benefit of Y — **Active** · intensity 3 (Intense)

By stripping authorship from the originator, the tactic directly cuts the reputational payoff the originator would have received for producing the win. The work itself still occurred, but its career value to the producer collapses — the visibility, the executive sponsorship, the next-assignment leverage all flow elsewhere.

#### C(Y)↑ — Amplify cost of Y — **Active** · intensity 3 (Intense)

The 'demoralize the originators' clause names the cost side explicitly. Repeated theft of credit raises the psychological and motivational cost of continuing to produce — learned helplessness sets in (Seligman) and the originator either reduces output, leaves for an environment with cleaner attribution, or retreats into defensive credit-marking that itself reads as political and costs them politically. Continuing to produce inside the rigged system becomes increasingly expensive.

### Strategy

**#13 Y-Focused Triple** · `B(X)↑ + B(Y)↓ + C(Y)↑`

**Rationale.** Credit is a near-zero-sum reputational currency in most organizations — there is only so much executive attention to attach to any given win. Pulling all three of B(X)↑, B(Y)↓, C(Y)↑ from the same act of misattribution is what makes the tactic efficient: one rewriting of the narrative simultaneously inflates the senior, deflates the rival, and bleeds the rival's stamina. C(X)↓ is absent because the senior is not trying to make their own choice easier — they are trying to make their own standing larger.

**Effectiveness factors.** Works when (a) the executive audience consumes the work through summaries rather than direct observation, (b) the senior controls the upward channel — pre-reads, decks, status updates, exec one-on-ones, (c) the originator lacks an independent advocate at the senior's level, and (d) organizational culture treats public credit-claiming by the producer as 'unprofessional' or 'political,' which silences the natural counter. Fails when the originator has cultivated direct executive exposure, when written artifacts pre-date the senior's involvement, or when a third-party witness with credibility breaks the attribution monopoly.

## Generic Analysis

### Influence Mechanism

**Psychological Principle.** Goffman's Impression Management combined with Akerlof's Information Asymmetry on attribution. Credit-poaching exploits the gap between what an audience can directly observe (the outcome) and what they can verify (who actually produced it). The actor stages a 'front' of authorship to a credit-consuming audience that has no economical way to audit attribution, while the producer — the only party who could correct the record — is held in an information-poor position downstream of the actor.

**Decision Alteration.** The audience's reward decision shifts because the audience does not perceive a competition between the actor and the originator — they perceive a single salient producer (the actor) and reward accordingly. Concurrently, the originator's go-forward decision (whether to keep producing high-quality work, whether to stay) is shifted because the link between effort and reputational return has been severed; rational actors disinvest from systems where their output is appropriated.

**Optimal Conditions.** Most effective in environments with (a) high collaborative production, where attribution is genuinely ambiguous, (b) hierarchical upward-reporting where intermediate layers control what the apex sees, (c) cultural norms against self-promotion by producers, and (d) audiences who reward narratives rather than artifacts. Less effective in environments with strong attribution infrastructure — version control, public authorship, named patents, peer-witnessed demos.

### Representative Scenario 1

**Context:** A consumer-goods company where the division head presents quarterly results directly to the CEO using a deck the product manager built, with the manager not invited into the room. The division head reframes the program as 'something I championed' and the manager learns about the framing third-hand weeks later.

**Sample X:** The division head is credited by the CEO with originating a customer-retention program that produced a 12-point NPS jump

**Sample Y:** The mid-level product manager who actually designed and ran the program is credited and promoted

**Template levers:**

- B(X)↑ ✓ — Attaches a measurable, attributable win (NPS jump) directly to the division head's executive narrative, materially strengthening their case for the next promotion cycle and the next budget round

- C(X)↓ ✗ — Does not reduce the friction of the division head's standing — that standing was not friction-constrained; it was visibility-constrained

- B(Y)↓ ✓ — The product manager's reputational payoff for the win is hollowed out — the executive layer never associates the result with their name, so the work generates no career-grade currency for them

- C(Y)↑ ✓ — Each future high-quality project becomes psychologically more expensive for the product manager to invest in, because the attribution path has been demonstrated to terminate elsewhere; producing well costs the same effort but yields less

### Representative Scenario 2

**Context:** An academic lab where the principal investigator routinely lists themselves as corresponding author and reframes lab meetings' insights as their own in conference keynotes. The postdoc's contributions appear in the paper but are invisible in the keynote circuit where faculty searches actually pay attention.

**Sample X:** The senior researcher is awarded the named professorship for breakthrough work on a protein folding model

**Sample Y:** The postdoctoral fellow who conceived and executed the key methodological move retains first-authorship and the visibility to land a faculty offer

**Template levers:**

- B(X)↑ ✓ — Conference keynotes and reference letters compound the PI's reputation as the originator of a methodological school, increasing tenure prestige, grant flow, and recruitment pull

- C(X)↓ ✗ — The PI's institutional standing is not held back by friction; it is held back only by what they can credibly claim, which the tactic adjusts directly

- B(Y)↓ ✓ — Faculty search committees evaluate candidates partly on whether they are 'known as' the source of their ideas — when the PI is the public face, the postdoc's identical CV line item carries far less weight

- C(Y)↑ ✓ — The postdoc faces a brutal choice: confront the PI (which damages reference letters they need for the job market) or absorb the loss (which forecloses on faculty positions). Either path is costly; producing under those terms is increasingly expensive

### Versatility

**Level:** high

**Explanation.** Operates in any context with three structural features: (1) collaborative or hierarchical production where attribution is ambiguous, (2) an audience that rewards perceived authorship rather than directly-observed contribution, and (3) intermediation between producer and audience. Found in corporate strategy teams, academic labs, government policy units, consulting engagements, family businesses, religious organizations, and creative industries. Less effective in artifact-rich environments — open-source software (git blame), patented inventions (named inventors), peer-witnessed performance — where attribution infrastructure constrains the move.

### Core Strategy

**#13 Y-Focused Triple** · `B(X)↑ + B(Y)↓ + C(Y)↑`

### Application Examples

- A management consultant partner presents a client's leadership team with a strategy framework that was developed primarily by an associate, with the associate left off the deck and the partner taking the relationship-defining client meeting alone
- A senate staff director takes public authorship of a bill's central provisions in committee press releases and Sunday-show appearances, while the legislative aide who drafted the language is named only in internal acknowledgments
- A founding family member at a multi-generational family business announces the new operating model at the annual offsite without naming the non-family executive who designed it, framing it as 'my modernization initiative' to reassure the older generation
- A senior sales VP forwards a junior account executive's deal memo to the CEO with only the VP's name in the from-line and a 'thought you'd want to see what we're working on' note, capturing executive credit for the strategy that produced the win
- A bishop in a religious organization claims the conception of a successful community outreach program during a synod address, while the parish priest who designed and implemented it sits in the audience without attribution
- A general officer takes credit in war-college lectures and memoir for an innovative counter-insurgency doctrine actually developed by a junior staff officer whose name appears only in obscure operational reports

## Integrated Insights

### Pattern Recognition

The specific and generic analyses converge on the same lever configuration — Polarization Triple (B(X)↑ + B(Y)↓ + C(Y)↑) — which is itself informative. Whether the producer is a 'political opponent' inside a factional struggle or simply a subordinate without independent executive access, the tactic operates identically on the credit-economy. The factional framing in the description adds intent (the senior wants the opponent demoralized), but the mechanism does not require malice toward the producer to work — indifference plus opportunity is sufficient.

### Strategic Implications

When to use the specific lens: Apply the specific lens when diagnosing tactics inside an active factional contest where the choice of victim is non-random — i.e., the senior is poaching specifically from rivals because the demoralization is the point, not merely a side-effect. Counter-strategy in this lens runs through coalition-building: the originator needs an executive sponsor outside the senior's faction who can independently verify attribution. When to use the generic lens: Apply the generic lens when the producer is being poached not because they are a political target but because they are structurally exposed — junior, off-camera, dependent on the senior for upward channels. Counter-strategy here is structural rather than political: build artifact-trails (dated documents, version control, witnessed demos), cultivate direct executive exposure, and select for organizations with named-author cultures.

### Resolution Approach

Credit poaching is a status play, not a switching play, which is why it skips C(X)↓ and lands on the Polarization Triple — the senior is not trying to be easier to choose, they are trying to be larger in the audience's perception while the producer becomes smaller and more tired. The tactic's signature is the asymmetry: one act of narrative misattribution does three things simultaneously, which is what makes it efficient and, for ambitious mediocrities, addictive. The defense is therefore not to argue the credit publicly (which costs the producer more than the senior) but to engineer attribution infrastructure that makes the move structurally harder to execute in the first place.

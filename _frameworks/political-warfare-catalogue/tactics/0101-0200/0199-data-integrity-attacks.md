# 0199 — DATA INTEGRITY ATTACKS

> Systematically questioning the validity, methodology, or source of internal data that contradicts the transformation narrative, forcing reliance on externally sourced or leadership-sanctioned information.

**Type:** `hybrid` · **Strategy:** **#14 Resistance Triple** · `C(X)↓ + B(Y)↓ + C(Y)↑` · **Cluster:** C05 — Data Manipulation & Metric Engineering

**Cited principles:** Akerlof, Janis, Hovland

---

## Classification Rationale

**Ambiguity Source.** The description names a specific organizational context — the transformation narrative being defended against contradictory internal data — but the operational move (systematically attacking the provenance and methodology of inconvenient evidence to force reliance on a curated source) is a context-independent epistemic-authority play used everywhere from courtrooms to intelligence agencies to scientific disputes.

**Specific Interpretation.** Reads as a transformation-period defensive tactic where change leadership, faced with internal data (legacy metrics, frontline reports, operational dashboards) that undermine the new narrative, attacks the data's validity, methodology, or source rather than its conclusions — channeling decisions toward externally-sourced or leadership-blessed information that confirms the desired direction.

**Generic Interpretation.** Reads as a broader influence pattern of epistemic gatekeeping: discrediting whole classes of evidence at the source so the contest of ideas is fought only on terrain where the actor controls what counts as a fact. The mechanism is identical whether the contested data is a sales report, an environmental study, or an intelligence assessment.

**Primary Classification.** generic

**Reasoning.** The transformation context is the showcase example, but the underlying move — using methodological challenges as a proxy weapon against unwelcome conclusions — is one of the oldest and most reusable plays in any environment where decisions are justified by data. The mechanism is what travels; the transformation framing is one of countless mountings.

---

## Specific Analysis

### Choice Architecture

- **Option X:** Decision-makers rely on externally-sourced or leadership-sanctioned data when evaluating transformation progress and direction
- **Option Y:** Decision-makers weigh internal operational data, frontline reports, and legacy metrics that contradict the transformation narrative
- **Context:** A transformation in flight where leadership has publicly committed to a new direction, but internal operational data (productivity, customer satisfaction, error rates, attrition) is producing readings inconsistent with the promised improvements, threatening sponsor confidence and political cover

### Active Levers

#### B(X)↑ — Amplify benefit of X — Not active

The tactic does not make leadership-sanctioned data more substantively valuable or insightful — it does not enhance the analytic merit of the preferred source. The preferred source wins by becoming the only surviving option, not by becoming more compelling on its own terms. Marking this lever active would conflate 'last man standing' with 'most attractive choice.'

#### C(X)↓ — Reduce cost of X — **Active** · intensity 3 (Intense)

Once leadership-sanctioned data is established as the trusted source, relying on it becomes the cognitively and politically cheap path: no need to triangulate, no need to defend a methodology choice, no need to explain why one is citing a 'discredited' internal system. The decision-maker is spared the cost of independent verification, and the actor pre-clears the audit trail.

#### B(Y)↓ — Diminish benefit of Y — **Active** · intensity 3 (Intense)

This is a central operating lever. By systematically questioning the validity, methodology, or source of the contradicting internal data, the tactic destroys the data's epistemic value before its content can be debated. Whatever signal the internal numbers carry is rendered moot because the source itself is no longer credible — a Spence-style signaling collapse where the sender's credibility, not the message's accuracy, decides what gets heard.

#### C(Y)↑ — Amplify cost of Y — **Active** · intensity 3 (Intense)

Anyone who cites the contradicting internal data now bears the burden of defending its methodology against publicly-aired doubts — a fight they did not choose, on unfavorable terrain, with the suspicion that they are personally invested in a 'flawed' system. The political cost of championing 'broken' data falls heavily on the analyst, the line manager, or the dissenter, often deterring the citation before it occurs.

### Strategy

**#14 Resistance Triple** · `C(X)↓ + B(Y)↓ + C(Y)↑`

**Rationale.** The tactic wins not by promoting the sanctioned data's merits but by making the contradicting data untouchable. Three coordinated moves — making the preferred source frictionless to use, stripping the alternative source of credibility, and loading social/political cost onto anyone who still cites the alternative — collapse the field of admissible evidence around the narrative leadership wants to defend.

**Effectiveness factors.** Effective when (a) the internal data systems have any genuine methodological imperfections — and they always do, so the attack always has a foothold; (b) the audience lacks the technical depth to adjudicate the methodology critique on its merits and defaults to 'where there's smoke there's fire'; (c) the actor has the standing to commission the external/sanctioned replacement source. Fails when an independent data steward (CFO function, internal audit, regulator) has both the technical depth and the political independence to publicly defend the contested internal source.

## Generic Analysis

### Influence Mechanism

**Psychological Principle.** Akerlof's Information Asymmetry & 'Lemons' Problem combined with Source Credibility Bias (Hovland/Janis). Once doubt is cast on a class of evidence, the cost of distinguishing the good signals from the bad inside that class becomes prohibitive, and the rational audience discounts the entire pool — exactly as buyers do in the used-car market when they cannot tell lemons from peaches.

**Decision Alteration.** Reshapes the decision not by arguing the substantive question but by collapsing the evidence base on which the substantive question would be answered. The audience never gets to weigh the contradicting data's content because the data has already been marked as untrustworthy at the source. The actor's preferred conclusion wins by default — it is the only one still supported by 'reliable' evidence after the cull.

**Optimal Conditions.** Works best when (a) the audience is not the data's producer and lacks first-hand knowledge of its provenance, (b) methodological complexity gives plausible cover for source-doubt without requiring proof of actual error, (c) the actor controls or can credibly sponsor an alternative 'clean' data source, and (d) decisions are made under time pressure where rebuilding evidence from scratch is not feasible.

### Representative Scenario 1

**Context:** A struggling public company where the CEO needs the board to ratify a costly pivot; the CFO's internal numbers are showing the existing business is healthier than the pivot narrative implies, and the CEO begins publicly questioning the CFO function's 'outdated chart of accounts' and 'allocation methodologies' in board sessions

**Sample X:** The board accepts the CEO's externally-commissioned strategic review as the authoritative read on company performance

**Sample Y:** The board weighs the CFO's internal monthly operating reports alongside the external review

**Template levers:**

- B(X)↑ ✗ — The external review does not become more analytically rigorous — it merely becomes the surviving option after internal data is delegitimized.

- C(X)↓ ✓ — The board can endorse the external review without engaging the underlying disagreement, having been given pre-packaged cover that the internal data is 'methodologically suspect.'

- B(Y)↓ ✓ — The CFO's reports lose their decision-grade status; even genuine signals in the internal numbers are perceived as products of the criticized methodology and discounted accordingly.

- C(Y)↑ ✓ — Board members who cite the CFO's reports must now justify why they trust a system the CEO has flagged as flawed — a position that risks looking unsophisticated or aligned with the wrong faction.

### Representative Scenario 2

**Context:** A regulated industry facing the prospect of stricter rules systematically funds methodological critiques of the academic and government studies — questioning sample sizes, statistical models, peer review processes — published in adjacent journals and circulated to agency staff, while offering its own well-funded 'rigorous' studies as the alternative

**Sample X:** A regulatory agency's rulemaking relies on industry-funded studies the regulated party has supplied

**Sample Y:** The rulemaking incorporates findings from academic and government-lab studies showing the regulated activity is harmful

**Template levers:**

- B(X)↑ ✗ — The industry studies are not made more scientifically meritorious by this tactic — only made into the cleaner-looking option after rivals have been muddied.

- C(X)↓ ✓ — Agency staff can cite industry data without having to defend a contested choice — the industry has pre-built the methodological case for why its source is the trustworthy one.

- B(Y)↓ ✓ — Once the academic studies are tagged as 'methodologically debated,' their evidentiary weight in the rulemaking record drops sharply, regardless of how robust the findings actually are.

- C(Y)↑ ✓ — Agency staff or commissioners who lean on the academic studies face industry-funded comments and litigation challenging the studies' admissibility — turning routine reliance into a defendable position.

### Versatility

**Level:** high

**Explanation.** Applies to any decision arena where conclusions are justified by reference to data sources and where the audience cannot independently verify provenance — corporate boardrooms, regulatory rulemaking, litigation, intelligence assessments, scientific controversy, electoral disputes, and academic peer review. Less effective in domains with strong independent data stewards (e.g., audited financial statements with public auditor liability) or where the audience has direct first-hand knowledge of the data's production.

### Core Strategy

**#14 Resistance Triple** · `C(X)↓ + B(Y)↓ + C(Y)↑`

### Application Examples

- Corporate: A new CEO systematically questions the prior regime's customer-satisfaction methodology so that legacy NPS readings can no longer be cited against the strategy reset.
- Government/Regulatory: Industry-funded methodological critiques of EPA and academic environmental studies during rulemaking, paired with industry-sponsored 'sound science' replacements.
- Litigation: Defense counsel attacking the chain-of-custody and lab protocols on prosecution evidence to render forensic findings inadmissible rather than contesting the underlying conclusion.
- Intelligence: A policy faction discrediting the analytic tradecraft of agencies producing unwelcome assessments while elevating outside 'red teams' whose findings align with preferred policy.
- Academic: A senior scholar dismissing a rival school's quantitative findings as 'p-hacked' or 'using a contested instrument,' steering the field's citations toward studies using the senior scholar's preferred methods.
- Activism/NGO: Fossil-fuel and tobacco strategies of seeding doubt about climate or epidemiological data sources so that policymakers default to industry-sanctioned figures.

## Integrated Insights

### Pattern Recognition

The two analyses converge cleanly on Strategy 14 (Resistance Triple). Both the specific transformation reading and the generic epistemic-gatekeeping reading agree that the tactic does not promote its preferred source's benefit — it wins by making the alternative source unusable while keeping the preferred path frictionless. This convergence is itself diagnostic: tactics that attack evidence rather than promote conclusions almost always show this exact lever signature.

### Strategic Implications

When to use the specific lens: Use the specific lens when advising a leader inside a transformation, an executive sponsor whose data is being attacked, or an internal auditor whose function is being delegitimized — the specific reading surfaces the local political stakes (sponsor cover, narrative coherence, the political vulnerability of internal data stewards) that the generic reading abstracts away. When to use the generic lens: Use the generic lens when the analyst needs to recognize the same move across very different domains — regulatory capture, scientific controversy, litigation tactics, intelligence disputes — and design a counter-strategy that is structural (independent data stewardship, pre-committed methodology, public methodology documentation) rather than narrative. cluster_differentiation: Within Cluster C05 (Data Manipulation & Metric Engineering), this tactic is distinguished from siblings by its target: it attacks the credibility of the data source itself, not the data's structure (DASHBOARD DIPLOMACY hides inside presentation), not the definitions (CRITERIA SHIFTING, DEFINITION OF DONE WARFARE move the goalposts), not the availability (DATA DESERT CREATION removes data), and not the interpretation (CONTEXT-FREE DATA DUMP overwhelms with raw material). DATA INTEGRITY ATTACKS is the cluster's purest epistemic-delegitimization play — the others manipulate the data game, this one disqualifies a player from the game.

### Resolution Approach

The deepest insight is that this tactic almost never argues against the conclusion the contested data supports — it argues against the data's right to be heard at all. The counter-move, therefore, is not to defend the conclusion harder but to make the data source structurally undelegitimizable in advance: independent governance, pre-registered methodologies, third-party audit, public documentation. Once the methodology fight has started in earnest, the data has usually already lost — the cost of rebuilding credibility under attack is far higher than the cost of pre-committing to defensible methods before there was anything to defend.

## Data Integrity Attack Dynamics

**Mechanism Signature.** Source-level delegitimization that pre-empts content-level engagement. The attack is on provenance, methodology, or instrument, never on the substantive conclusion the contested data supports.

**Escalation Pattern.** Begins with isolated questions in low-stakes settings ('I wonder how that's measured'), escalates to commissioned methodological reviews, then to public delegitimization in formal decision forums, then to replacement of the contested source with a sanctioned alternative. Each step makes the previous step harder to reverse.

**Counter Measures.** Pre-commit methodologies before they are contested. Establish independent data stewardship (function reports to audit committee, not to operating leadership). Publish methodology documentation so the attack must engage on technical merits. Maintain a public chain of methodology decisions with dated approvals — turning the attack into an attack on previously-ratified governance, which is politically costlier.

**Exit Criteria.** The tactic exhausts its utility once an independent authority (auditor, regulator, court, external academic body) endorses the contested source's methodology. After that, continued attack reads as bad faith and damages the attacker's own credibility.

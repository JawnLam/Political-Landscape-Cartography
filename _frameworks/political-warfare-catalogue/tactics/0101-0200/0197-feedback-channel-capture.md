# 0197 — FEEDBACK CHANNEL CAPTURE

> Controlling the mechanisms for employee feedback (surveys, town halls, suggestion boxes) to filter out significant dissent, ensuring that only positive or manageable views reach senior decision-makers.

**Type:** `hybrid` · **Strategy:** **#15 Full Spectrum** · `B(X)↑ + C(X)↓ + B(Y)↓ + C(Y)↑` · **Cluster:** C07 — Echo Chambers & Feedback Filtering

**Cited principles:** Goffman, Akerlof

---

## Classification Rationale

**Ambiguity Source.** The tactic names a concrete set of organizational artifacts (engagement surveys, town halls, suggestion boxes) but its mechanism — capturing the instruments that produce 'the voice of the people' so the curator controls what that voice says — is a general principle of information warfare visible from polling firms to authoritarian regimes.

**Specific Interpretation.** An HR function, communications team, or middle-management layer designs and administers the formal feedback machinery so that survey items, town hall question screening, and suggestion-box triage systematically filter out dissent before it reaches the C-suite or board.

**Generic Interpretation.** Whoever controls the measurement instrument controls the measured reality; capturing the channel between a population and its decision-makers grants the curator de facto editorial authority over what the decision-maker perceives as legitimate sentiment.

**Primary Classification.** generic

**Reasoning.** The 'feedback channels' label is incidental — the same mechanism operates in polling, intelligence summaries, board packs, customer-research reports, and journalistic gatekeeping. The defining feature is the structural position between source and decision-maker, not the specific HR artifacts named.

---

## Specific Analysis

### Choice Architecture

- **Option X:** Senior leadership receives a curated, filtered, mostly-positive read of employee sentiment that confirms current strategy
- **Option Y:** Senior leadership receives raw, unflattering signal — including hostile open-text comments, dissenting town hall questions, and unpopular suggestion-box themes
- **Context:** A large organization where employee voice is collected through formal instruments (annual engagement survey, all-hands Q&A, pulse polls, ethics hotline, suggestion box) administered by HR, Communications, or Internal Comms — functions that report through layers that have their own interest in how the data lands

### Active Levers

#### B(X)↑ — Amplify benefit of X — **Active** · intensity 3 (Intense)

The filtered report is engineered to look like 'good news' — high engagement scores, themes of 'gratitude' and 'pride,' carefully selected verbatims. This actively boosts the perceived benefit of the curated view to senior leaders, who experience it as validation of their strategy and of the HR function delivering it. The filterer is rewarded with credibility as a steady, professional partner.

#### C(X)↓ — Reduce cost of X — **Active** · intensity 3 (Intense)

Filtering is made structurally cheap: forced-choice Likert scales suppress open-ended dissent, town hall questions are pre-submitted and screened, suggestion-box themes are summarized by the same people whose departments would be implicated, and survey vendors offer 'executive summaries' that aggregate dissent into a single 'areas for development' bullet. Each act of filtering is one editorial choice inside an accepted methodology, not a visible act of suppression.

#### B(Y)↓ — Diminish benefit of Y — **Active** · intensity 3 (Intense)

Raw dissent is pre-discredited as 'unconstructive feedback,' 'venting,' 'a vocal minority,' or 'outliers.' Open-text comments are characterized as anecdotal noise next to the 'statistically valid' aggregate scores. The benefit of surfacing honest signal is attacked at the categorical level — leadership is taught not to trust the unfiltered view even when shown it.

#### C(Y)↑ — Amplify cost of Y — **Active** · intensity 3 (Intense)

An employee, manager, or analyst who tries to pass raw dissent upward bypasses the captured channel — making them visibly disloyal to the curator, exposing them to retaliation (PIPs, reorganization out of the role, exclusion from future surveys), and marking them as a poor 'team player' who 'amplifies negativity.' The town hall question that wasn't pre-screened becomes a career event for whoever asked it.

### Strategy

**#15 Full Spectrum** · `B(X)↑ + C(X)↓ + B(Y)↓ + C(Y)↑`

**Rationale.** Channel capture is unusual in genuinely operating all four levers because it is structural rather than situational. By owning the instrument, the curator simultaneously rewards the filtered view (B(X)↑ — leaders feel good, curator looks competent), makes filtering the default workflow (C(X)↓ — it's just 'how we run engagement'), pre-discredits raw signal as a category (B(Y)↓), and punishes anyone who routes around the channel (C(Y)↑). The four levers are not stacked features; they are the natural byproducts of holding the gatekeeping position.

**Effectiveness factors.** Effective when (a) the captured channel is the only formal mechanism — no parallel skip-level conversations, anonymous external whistleblower lines, or board-direct access; (b) the senior decision-maker has no first-hand contact with the frontline (geographic distance, hierarchical insulation, 'royal court' insulation by chief of staff); (c) the curator's incentives are tied to engagement scores rather than signal quality. Fails when an outside event (regulatory probe, glassdoor leak, activist short report, mass resignation) produces a raw counter-reading that exposes the gap between the captured channel's story and reality.

## Generic Analysis

### Influence Mechanism

**Psychological Principle.** Akerlof's Information Asymmetry combined with Goffman's frame analysis — the curator occupies the position of asymmetric information advantage between source and decision-maker, and the methodological frame ('survey,' 'town hall,' 'data') is treated as neutral measurement when it is in fact a designed instrument whose every choice (item wording, question screening, summarization, exclusion thresholds) is editorial.

**Decision Alteration.** Decision-makers do not experience themselves as receiving a filtered version of reality; they experience themselves as receiving 'the data.' This shifts the entire decision frame from 'what do my people actually think?' to 'what does the engagement report say?' — and the latter is fully under the curator's control. The decision-maker's confidence in their read of the organization rises while the read's correspondence to ground truth falls.

**Optimal Conditions.** Works where the decision-maker is structurally distant from the source (large organization, hierarchical insulation, geographic spread), where formal measurement carries epistemic prestige ('the survey says...'), and where alternative channels for the source-population's voice are absent or low-status. Information-asymmetry is most extractable when the source-population itself trusts the channel — believing they are being heard sustains participation that feeds the captured instrument.

### Representative Scenario 1

**Context:** A political campaign where the in-house pollster controls the methodology of internal tracking polls. The pollster's bonus is tied to keeping the candidate's confidence high through the final two weeks. Likely-voter screens, weighting decisions, and topline framing are all editorial choices the candidate experiences as neutral measurement.

**Sample X:** The polling firm reports the candidate is leading by 4 points based on 'likely voter' methodology adjustments

**Sample Y:** The campaign manager learns the underlying raw data shows the candidate trailing by 6 among walk-in voters who refuse phone polls

**Template levers:**

- B(X)↑ ✓ — The favorable topline boosts the pollster's standing inside the campaign and protects the candidate's morale, which produces better performance, which retroactively justifies the methodology.

- C(X)↓ ✓ — Methodological filtering hides inside accepted polling practice — 'likely voter screens,' 'demographic weighting,' 'house effects' are all defensible choices the campaign manager cannot easily second-guess.

- B(Y)↓ ✓ — Raw unweighted numbers are characterized as 'unrigorous,' 'a snapshot,' or 'reflecting nonresponse bias' — pre-discrediting any leaked alternative read.

- C(Y)↑ ✓ — A junior analyst who circulates the raw crosstabs is breaching pollster control, can be fired for 'methodological irresponsibility,' and earns a reputation for disloyalty that follows them to the next campaign.

### Representative Scenario 2

**Context:** A national intelligence director controls the synthesis and ordering of inputs from multiple collection streams (HUMINT, SIGINT, station reports). Career analysts whose raw reports contradict the agency's institutional read have their assessments downgraded to footnotes or excluded from the executive summary. The President never reads the raw cables.

**Sample X:** The intelligence summary delivered in the President's Daily Brief presents the regime as stable with manageable unrest

**Sample Y:** The PDB presents the regime as approaching collapse with revolutionary momentum among the urban middle class

**Template levers:**

- B(X)↑ ✓ — The 'stable regime' read aligns with current diplomatic strategy and protects the intelligence director's standing with the White House — the curator is rewarded for delivering an actionable, decision-ready read.

- C(X)↓ ✓ — Filtering hides inside the legitimate craft of intelligence synthesis — analytic tradecraft norms permit downgrading 'uncorroborated' reports, deprioritizing 'low-confidence' sources, and excluding 'analytic outliers' from the executive product.

- B(Y)↓ ✓ — The dissenting analyst's read is characterized as 'an outlier,' 'lacking corroboration,' or 'overweighting anecdotal HUMINT' — its informational value is attacked at the methodological level rather than the substantive one.

- C(Y)↑ ✓ — The analyst who tries to brief the President directly, or leaks to a congressional committee, faces career termination, security clearance review, and prosecution — the cost of routing around the captured channel is existential.

### Versatility

**Level:** high

**Explanation.** Applies anywhere a single curator or function controls the formal pipeline between a source population and a decision-maker who lacks direct access — corporate engagement surveys, political polling, intelligence briefings, customer research reports to product leadership, board pack assembly, regulatory commentary submissions, peer review committees, and editorial gatekeeping in journalism. Less effective in flat or transparent environments where the decision-maker has direct unmediated contact with the source (small businesses, founder-led startups, partner-led firms with skip-level norms).

### Core Strategy

### Application Examples

- Corporate: An engagement-survey vendor relationship managed by the CHRO's team, where open-text comments are themed by HR analysts whose departments would be implicated by negative themes, before the board sees a 12-slide deck of green dashboards.
- Government: A national security advisor controlling the order and emphasis of the PDB so that intelligence contradicting the administration's preferred narrative arrives as a low-confidence footnote rather than the lead.
- Sales: An enterprise account team filtering customer satisfaction calls so that the renewal committee hears the three happiest references and never the seven accounts in active escalation.
- Academic: A department chair controlling student-evaluation summaries and exit-survey reporting to the dean, so that complaints about teaching quality are aggregated into a single 'curriculum improvement opportunity' line item rather than named courses.
- Lobbying: A trade association running 'member sentiment' polls whose methodology is designed to produce the policy position the executive director already holds, then presenting the result to legislators as the industry's voice.
- Religious organization: A diocesan communications office curating which letters and concerns from parishes reach the bishop's desk, so that systemic complaints about a specific pastor arrive only as isolated incidents.
- NGO: A program-evaluation function controlling beneficiary survey design so that donor reports show high impact scores while field staff observations of program failure stay in unread internal channels.

## Integrated Insights

### Pattern Recognition

Both readings produce Full Spectrum (Strategy 15), and this convergence is itself the insight — channel capture is one of the few tactics that genuinely operates all four levers because it is structural rather than situational. The curator does not need to deploy separate moves to activate each lever; holding the channel position activates them automatically. This distinguishes it from the cluster's siblings: THE SYCOPHANCY TRAP cultivates a relational pattern around the leader; THE 'IVORY TOWER' EFFECT is a passive insulation by distance; THE 'NO BAD NEWS' IMPERATIVE is a norm enforced through reaction. FEEDBACK CHANNEL CAPTURE is the engineered infrastructure that makes those softer dynamics possible — the formal apparatus that ratifies whatever the informal court would have wanted.

### Strategic Implications

When to use the specific lens: When advising a board, audit committee, or incoming CEO who wants to know whether they are receiving an honest read of their organization. The specific lens names the artifacts to inspect (survey design, town hall screening process, suggestion-box triage, exit interview methodology) and the structural fix (parallel channels, third-party administration with raw data direct to the board, skip-level access, anonymous external whistleblower line reporting to audit committee rather than HR). When to use the generic lens: When analyzing any decision-maker's epistemic environment — political principal and pollster, executive and intelligence director, fund manager and research analyst, regulator and industry submissions. The generic lens makes visible that the question 'is this person well-informed?' is always also the question 'who curates the channel?' — a question the decision-maker is structurally disinclined to ask because the curator's product feels like ground truth.

### Resolution Approach

The defining move of channel capture is not falsification but methodology — the curator does not lie about the data, they own the rules by which data becomes 'the data.' The countermeasure is therefore not better feedback but redundant channels: any decision-maker who has only one pipeline to a source population has, by definition, given that pipeline's curator editorial control over their perception of reality, and no amount of integrity on the curator's part neutralizes the structural conflict of interest.

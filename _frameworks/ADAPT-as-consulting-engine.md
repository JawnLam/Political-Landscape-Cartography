# ADAPT as Consulting Engine — Craft Layer

> **The Methodology Author's 42-page ADAPT Loop Field Manual decoded into operational craft for AI deployment inside PLC engagements.** This file extracts what the manual teaches into something the AI can deploy when coaching the principal, diagnosing stuck states, scoring decision matrices, and tracking perception gaps across cycles. Source: `sources/ADAPT-Loop-Field-Manual.pdf` (42 pages, 4 views + 3 case studies).
>
> Where the lookup card `ADAPT.md` is the 5-beat reference, this file is the deployment manual — how to actually USE ADAPT in conversation with a principal, where to recognize specific stuck states, and how the framework's quiet but central claims (the perception gap, the bets-vs-workhorses matrix discipline, the No-loop as strategic asset) operate in practice.

## The 4 Views — what each one is for

The master ADAPT diagram is dense by design. The Field Manual decodes it into four progressively-zoomed views. The AI uses different views in different coaching moments:

| View | What it shows | When the AI uses it |
|------|---------------|---------------------|
| **View 1 — The whole loop** | 5 beats in 2 zones (planning + consequence) | First conversation; orienting a new principal; diagnosing which beat the principal is stuck in |
| **View 2 — The planning funnel** | 6 layers: Actor → Agenda → Interest → Objective → Goal → Target | Drafting the principal's funnel; sharpening a vague want into a movable target |
| **View 3 — The decision** | Capability gate + Decision Matrix with B/C/U per option | RUN-CYCLE step 4-9; gate evaluation; option scoring |
| **View 4 — Act / Perceive / Track** | 3 utilities (expected / actual / perceived) + perception gap + 2 feedback loops | Post-cycle reflection; ANALYZE-CYCLE; lesson extraction |

## The two zones — the framework's load-bearing asymmetry

The master diagram has two color zones the manual labels explicitly:

- **Planning zone** (Agenda + Decide) — inside the actor's head — **reversible**
- **Consequence zone** (Act + Perceive + Track) — out in the world — **irreversible**

The asymmetry is load-bearing. Every full ADAPT revolution moves something irrevocably from the actor's head into the world. Time in the planning zone is cheap; time recovering from an ill-considered move is expensive.

**The framework's implicit advice the AI should reinforce:** favor the cheap kind of time. When the principal seems to be skipping into action, slow down at Decide. When they're stuck in deliberation, distinguish "stuck deciding" from "stuck at the capability gate" — different remedies.

## View 2 — The 6-layer planning funnel

| Layer | What it is | Failure mode the AI watches for |
|-------|-----------|--------------------------------|
| **Actor** | The person or group capable of decisions | Misidentified actor — "the company" rarely decides; a particular executive does. Naming the actor wrong corrupts everything downstream. |
| **Agenda** | The long-running ambition the actor would still want if everything else changed | Rarely stated openly. Often misidentified by observers. An executive's agenda is rarely "to do my job well" — more likely "to be the person who gets to choose what gets done." Get the agenda wrong and every prediction about the actor drifts. |
| **Interest** | What the agenda produces at the moment | Situational and changeable. Losing an interest is recoverable; threatening an agenda is not. **Some situations escalate suddenly because someone perceives an attack on agenda when only an interest was at issue, or vice versa.** This is a major operator diagnostic. |
| **Objective** | A concrete aim that serves the interest | "Have more influence" is not an objective. "Win the seat on the steering committee" is. The transition from interest to objective is often where actors lose their way — feelings about wanting without forcing concrete formulation. |
| **Goal** | The specific measurable result that satisfies the objective | Where an objective can be described, a goal can be measured. "Get the seat" is the objective; "be one of the seven named members in the charter published next month" is the goal. |
| **Target** | The thing or person the actor must move to achieve the goal | The most-skipped layer. Targets are often the part of the situation an actor avoids naming clearly — because acknowledging a target forces a confrontation with the political reality of the goal. **Skipping target identification is one of the most common failure modes the framework exposes.** |

### The funnel runs one direction only

You cannot work backward from a target to an agenda. You cannot pick a goal and reverse-engineer the interest. The agenda is the prior; everything below is downstream.

**When an actor's behavior surprises you, the explanation almost always lies ABOVE the level you're observing.** A misread of someone's agenda produces wrong predictions about every objective and goal they pursue; a misread of their objective produces wrong predictions only about the current move. Distinguishing the two saves enormous time.

### Coaching application

When the principal describes their situation, the AI listens for which funnel layer is unclear. Common patterns:

- Principal articulates Interest but no Objective → ask: *"What would success look like that you could write down without ambiguity?"*
- Principal has Objective but no Goal → ask: *"What's the specific measurable result that would satisfy that?"*
- Principal has Goal but no Target → ask: *"Who controls whether that happens?"* (This is the question principals most resist; their resistance is itself the diagnostic.)
- Principal jumps to Move without funnel work → return them to Agenda: *"What's the long-running ambition this serves?"*

## View 3 — The decision (capability gate + matrix)

### The capability gate

Before any move can be made, the actor answers: **do I have enough resources?** Resources = everything spendable — cash, time, attention, political capital, relationships, credibility, information.

**Two legitimate responses to "No":**

1. **Rescope** — shrink the objective until it fits available resources
2. **Rebuild** — delay action and gather more resources

Both loop back into the planning funnel. **Neither is failure.**

**The failure mode** — and the one the AI watches for — is the principal plowing ahead anyway, hoping resources will materialize once action is underway. They generally do not.

The "No-loop" — disciplined rescope-or-rebuild — is the most strategically sophisticated position the framework can produce. Per the Reyes case study: Cycle 2 (the disciplined hold) is the cycle most practitioners skip and most situations require. Holding deliberately, with continued coalition-building underneath, is what allows a later cycle to be decisive rather than premature.

### The decision matrix

Each option scored across two scenarios × three measures = six cells per option:

|  | Preferred Position (PP) | Adverse Alternative (AA) |
|---|------------------------|-------------------------|
| **Benefit** | B(PP) | B(AA) |
| **Cost** | C(PP) | C(AA) |
| **Utility** | U(PP) = B(PP) − C(PP) | U(AA) = B(AA) − C(AA) |

**These are not "best case" and "worst case." They are the two outcomes the actor must actually plan for** — what success looks like AND the fallback that defines what failure costs.

### How to read the matrix — the framework's central discipline

**Not** by picking the option with the highest U(PP). That is a beginner's mistake.

The right reading: compare U(PP) and U(AA) together across all options. Ask: *"Which option's preferred position is most attractive given what its adverse alternative would cost me?"*

- **Option with brilliant PP and catastrophic AA = a bet, not a plan.** Bets cannot be run again and again.
- **Option with modest PP and tolerable AA = a workhorse.** Workhorses can be run repeatedly.

**Mature strategic action favors workhorses.** Bets are situational; workhorses compound.

The matrix is therefore not a soft decision aid — it is a **forcing function**. It requires the actor to articulate what failure looks like before committing to action. That articulation discipline is what separates running the cycle from improvising it.

### Coaching application

The AI surfaces three questions whenever the principal is in Decide:

1. *"What does the preferred position actually look like for this option? Describe it concretely."*
2. *"What's the adverse alternative — the realistic fallback, not the worst-case fantasy?"*
3. *"Can you score B, C, U for both? Even rough numbers — the discipline is in writing them down."*

When the principal balks at scoring (especially the cost columns), the AI gently presses. Per the Cathna case: founders systematically over-bet because they over-score U(PP) and waive away C(AA). The matrix's discipline is in the AA, not the PP.

## View 4 — The three utilities and the perception gap

After Act, three quantities the master diagram presents as if they were one:

| Utility | What it is | When it exists |
|---------|-----------|----------------|
| **Expected utility** | What the matrix predicted before action | Only in the actor's head, before action |
| **Actual utility** | What reality delivered, measured against the original target | Out in the world, regardless of what the actor believes |
| **Perceived utility** | What the actor believes they got | Filtered through hope, defensiveness, ego, prior belief |

### The perception gap

The most important quantity in the framework, and the one the master diagram does not draw, is the **perception gap = Actual utility − Perceived utility**.

It is the error term the actor will carry, unrecognized, into the next cycle.

**Most strategic mistakes are not failures of analysis but failures of perception.**

- Actor systematically OVER-perceives wins → over-invests in actions whose actual utility is lower than they think
- Actor systematically UNDER-perceives losses → fails to update interests when an update is overdue

### The two feedback loops

The cycle closes through two paths into the next round:

- **Resource loop** → back to Resources. Winners gain resources; losers spend them. Both start next cycle with different balance. Corrupted when the actor refuses to acknowledge a depleted balance.
- **Learning loop** → back to Interests. What the actor observed reshapes what they will care about next time. Corrupted when the actor's observation node is **starved** — distracted, occupied elsewhere, or filtered through too thick a layer of pre-existing belief.

**The starved observation node is a critical AI diagnostic.** An actor whose observation is starved can run the full cycle correctly in form but degrade over time because what they believe about the world is no longer being corrected by what is happening in it. The AI watches for principals whose attention has drifted to a parallel concern (an infrastructure migration, a personal crisis, a board fight elsewhere) and surfaces the risk explicitly: *"Your observation node is starved on this engagement. Either re-engage attention or accept that the next cycle's read will be unreliable."*

## The 5 stuck states — diagnostic framework

When the principal is stuck and can't name why, the AI runs this diagnostic:

| Stuck state | Feeling | Remedy |
|-------------|---------|--------|
| **Stuck in Agenda** | Restlessness without a target | Return to View 2 funnel; sharpen Interest → Objective → Goal → Target |
| **Stuck at the Decide gate** | Each attempt depletes resources you cannot afford to lose; keep starting and can't sustain | Capability check failed — rescope or rebuild; stop trying to push through |
| **Stuck deciding** | Paralysis dressed up as deliberation; too many options | Scoring discipline missing; force the matrix, especially the AA column |
| **Stuck in Perceive / Track** | Can't tell whether you won or lost | Observation node starved; rebuild attention; identify the actual-vs-perceived gap |

Each stuck state has a different remedy. **Distinguishing "I am stuck deciding" from "I am stuck at the capability gate" is itself a strategic move; the two produce opposite behaviors when treated the same way.**

## Case-study deployment patterns

The Field Manual provides 3 case studies that the AI can reference when coaching analogous situations.

### Case 1 — Reyes / Cresterly Industrial (corporate politics, 3 cycles)

**Setup:** SVP wants to absorb a peer's analytics group; needs CEO's eventual approval; faces entrenched CTO.

**Cycle 1 — The probe.** Workhorse selection (pilot, not frontal request). Actual lands closer to AA than PP (steering committee captured). Perception gap +2 carries into Cycle 2.

**Cycle 2 — The disciplined hold.** Capability gate returns No. Most practitioners would push; Reyes rescopes. Coalition-building continues. The CTO's observation node starves (parallel migration consuming attention).

**Cycle 3 — The strike.** Resources rebuilt; options changed; frontal becomes viable. Selection wins.

**The teach:** Cycle 2 is the cycle most practitioners skip and most situations require. The No-loop, used deliberately, sets up Cycle 3's success. Apparent randomness in outcomes (CEO's compromise terms) is usually another actor's matrix — the CTO's late, under-resourced cycle scored its AA correctly.

### Case 2 — Pelham / Cathna Series A (founder negotiation, 2 cycles)

**Setup:** Founder evaluating Series A term sheet under no-shop clock; one term sheet, two informal-interest firms, depleted runway.

**Cycle 1.** Target reframed from "Pinemark specifically" to "the market of Series A leads." Matrix exposes Option C (parallel process) as a bet not a plan. Workhorse Option B selected. Actual lands at +2 vs expected +3; perceived at +4 due to inflation around price concession that overshadowed failure to move structural terms.

**Cycle 2.** Outside option (Stelladin) emerges but unverified. The discipline: score for what it is — a possibility weighted by probability inside the timeline. Triggered withdrawal (Option E's AA) is intolerable. Sign.

**The teach:** Target = the market of counterparties, not the specific counterparty. Misidentification produces over-investment in any one negotiating partner. The matrix is more important when emotional stakes are higher; emotional inflation biases U(PP) up and waves away C(AA). Perception gaps compound across cycles — Cycle 1 inflation produces larger Cycle 2 inflation.

### Case 3 — Yarrow / Bellecorin CTO offer (personal career, 2 cycles)

**Setup:** Engineering director with stable senior role evaluating Series B startup CTO offer.

**Cycle 1.** **Target is the future actor.** Not the offer, not the company — the actor in 18 months looking back. Matrix forces honest weighting: equity denominated as probability distribution rather than headline number. Option A's B(PP) shrinks. Option B (negotiated equity-for-base trade) selected as marginal improvement. Unprompted concession on change-of-control acceleration materially improves AA.

**Cycle 2.** Revised matrix is finely balanced. The right reading: **whether the actor's agenda is served by the AA's cost being acceptable.** For Yarrow's agenda (be a technology executive whose judgment is structurally consequential), the AA of accepting (failed startup → 18-36 months as CTO → label/network/experience permanent → financial cost recoverable) serves the agenda. Option C (stay) satisfies the interest but not the agenda. Accept.

**The teach:** In personal career decisions, target = future actor, not the offer. The probability distribution of outcomes is more useful than the headline outcome value.

## How ADAPT layers with PLC

The two frames operate at different layers; PLC's `ADAPT.md` lookup card maps the basics. Adding here:

**ADAPT is the coaching frame; PLC is the political-landscape frame.** PLC answers: what is the sphere's political state? Who are the actors? Which moves are viable? What gates clear? ADAPT answers: how does the principal hold their agenda? How does decision-quality improve over cycles? How does the principal develop political mastery?

**The 14-constituent PLC interaction cycle maps to ADAPT thus:**

| ADAPT beat | PLC constituents | What's happening |
|-----------|------------------|------------------|
| A — Agenda | (cycle context: Actor + Agenda set at initiation; Interest formed as constituent 1) | Funnel work — sharpening from Actor through Target |
| D — Decide | Constituents 2-9 (Detection through capability-gate) + the two PLC-added gates | Capability check (constituent 9) + matrix scoring + ALTERNATIVES-AUDIT § 15 + ETHICAL-ACCOUNTING § 11 |
| A — Act | Constituent 10 (Execution) | The move enters the world |
| P — Perceive | Constituents 11-12 (Perception + Outcome) | Track-and-detect phase log |
| T — Track | Constituents 13-14 (Obtained Utility + Obtained Resource) + post-cycle Refinement + Lessons | Resource loop updates `[a./R.]`; learning loop updates Principal funnel sharpness + sphere state |

The perception gap discipline (View 4) is what the principal funnel sharpness scale (`plc_Principal_Funnel_Sharpness` 0-5) operationalizes for PLC. A principal at sharpness 4-5 closes the actual-vs-perceived gap consistently; one at sharpness 1-2 does not.

## Mode-aware deployment

**Consultant mode** — the consultant deploys the 4 Views in coaching conversations with the principal. The 5 stuck-state diagnostic runs at every cycle close. The 3 case studies serve as reference material when the principal's situation echoes one of the canonical patterns. Per OPC § 17 `advisor_engagement_politics` — the consultant's own ADAPT cycle runs in parallel at the engagement layer (consultant's Agenda = "deliver the engagement; develop the principal; preserve practitioner standing"; cycles match the engagement's cadence).

**Principal-self mode** — the operator runs ADAPT on themselves. No-false-confidence priming (per D12) means the AI surfaces perception-gap risk explicitly when the principal-self operator's reads sound suspiciously confident. The AI references View 4's perception-gap diagnostic by name when needed.

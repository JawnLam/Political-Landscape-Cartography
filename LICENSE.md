*Adjacent porous boundaries surfaced during pre-publication review that counsel may want to address explicitly during legal review:*

1. *Personal evaluation ↔ application threshold (addressed in this draft):* The fourteen-day window and the "burden of distinction rests on the individual" language tighten this. Counsel should test whether the language is enforceable in the chosen jurisdiction and whether the fourteen-day window is the right specific number.*

2. *Academic citation as cover for consulting deployment:* A practitioner may "cite" PLC in a published article while operationally deploying it in client engagements. The Conditions of All Permitted Use already exclude commercial benefit derived from PLC content (excepting properly cited academic publications), but the carve-out for academic publications could itself be exploited. Counsel may wish to define what constitutes a "properly cited academic publication" and what disqualifies a citation from the academic carve-out.*

3. *Knowledge once-read cannot be unread:* The license cannot legally erase the mental models a former evaluator carries forward. The license can require licensing for any DELIBERATE application; it cannot prevent informal residual influence on the former evaluator's thinking. Counsel should advise on whether this practical limitation needs to be acknowledged explicitly or whether the "deliberate application" framing is sufficient.*

4. *AI-mediated use:* Running the OV with an AI assistant on a personal account during the evaluation window — is the AI assistant's analysis on the individual's behalf personal evaluation or use? Counsel may want to address this explicitly given the methodology's design assumption that AI is the operating partner.*

5. *Hallway descriptions and informal knowledge transfer:* The Conditions exclude teaching or training, but the line between "describing PLC to a colleague over lunch" and "training a colleague to deploy PLC" is porous. Counsel may want to set a specific bright-line test (e.g., any structured walkthrough longer than thirty minutes constitutes training).*

6. *Multiple evaluation windows by the same person:* Whether a single individual can re-enter personal evaluation after the fourteen-day window expires (e.g., upon release of a new version) should be specified. The current draft is silent on this.*

7. *Organizational evaluation by individual proxies:* An employee may "personally evaluate" PLC on their own device but inevitably bring what they learned back to their organization. Counsel may want to address whether evaluation by an employee carries implicit organizational obligations.*
